### 55. (Documented, not scheduled) Multiplayer continuity — disconnects, rejoin, claimable slots
**Perceived:** a dropped player stops ruining the match. Depending on the lobby rule chosen: the game pauses and names who left, or a bot takes their army over, or a teammate inherits their units, budget and Supply Route. The larger version — someone joining a match already in progress, or a returning player reclaiming their army from the bot that held it — is a separate and far more expensive feature.
_**Written up 2026-08-12 at the user's request from a discussion, with the explicit instruction not to implement it now.** Artifact: [`plans/260812_multiplayer_continuity.md`](../../plans/260812_multiplayer_continuity.md). Nothing is due; the document exists so the decision is cheap when there is a reason._
_**The one thing to carry without re-reading it:** this is **two** features, not one. **Continuity** (a disconnect doesn't ruin the match) needs **nothing transferred** — every client already simulates the dropped player's units, budget and Supply Route, so only *who issues orders* changes. **Admission** (a client that wasn't there joins) requires bringing a new client to a bit-identical world state, and that is the entire bill. Both user scenarios collapse onto admission, so do not let the two be scoped as one: continuity ships on its own timeline._
_Second fact worth carrying: **admission is gated on determinism we have not demonstrated.** Restore is a replay from frame 0, not a snapshot — which is also why saved-game load is slow, and why that is architectural rather than optimisable. The user's stuck-pause bug WAS this: one synced field diverged on replay, the validating sync-hash check failed, and the game latched itself permanently unresumable. So the cheapest gate is not a feature but a **measurement** — get save→restore of a long match validating reliably on real maps first, and learn the answer for the price of a test run instead of a feature._
_More existed than expected: the server already records the full order stream (`Server.cs:136`, `:972`) and already knows each client's disconnect frame (`Server.cs:1269`); ownership transfer is broadly handled and `TemporaryOwnerManager` is **reversible**; lobby options are a cheap `ILobbyOptions` trait pattern; and only **2 of 54** bot modules initialise at `WorldLoaded`, so mid-match bot takeover is largely safe._
_Two traps flagged in the plan, repeated because they are easy to walk into: (a) any disconnect policy must decide what the dropped player *is* — lost / spectating / bot-driven / inherited — and that is the exact axis that produced the elimination-cascade bug fixed at `f49b6aca` two days before this was written; use its alliance-mask discipline rather than a fresh loop over players. (b) A "highest-scoring teammate" selector must read **synced** state or it is a desync, and the takeover moment must be an order in the stream, not a local decision._
_Balance note, which is the bigger question than the technical one: transferring a dropped player's army to a teammate makes a 2v2 into a 1v2 with doubled resources — frequently **stronger** than the bot option, and in team games that is an incentive to drop deliberately to consolidate. Conservative default (pause → bot), transfer opt-in, and implemented reversibly so a bad connection cannot permanently rob a returning player._
