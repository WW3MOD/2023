# RETHINK #2 — the next lever after the regime re-baseline

**Date:** 2026-07-21
**Mode:** READ-ONLY architecture review. No code changed, nothing built, no game run.
**Researched against:** `main` @ `90a173c4` (HEAD; the re-baseline that triggered this
was measured one commit earlier @ `bfa8e876` — see the TECN-ferry caveat in §0).
**Trigger:** ~5 behavior cycles elapsed AND the fresh regime re-baseline
(`runs/260721_regime_rebaseline.md`) shows **Experimental ≈ Stable on both rungs**.
**Charter for this doc:** DOCTRINE.md RETHINK — is the current structure still the
right vehicle, or is the next gain structural? Radical options costed for real
(DOCTRINE.md:25); incrementalism is a choice to be justified, not a default.

---

## Executive summary

The benchmark has caught up to the bot: Stable is the frozen snapshot of the last
validated Experimental config, and against it Experimental is dead-even (S1 5–5,
capture 6/10 vs 6/10; S2 5–5, swing edge −$350). The only Exp/Stable delta today —
the SR-contestation multiplier — beat a passive Normal by +$6,300 but is
neutral-to-negative against a competent same-faction Stable. More multiplier tuning
of that same axis is a dead end. **The next gain is structural, and the data points
at exactly the structural piece the user has already named the North Star: a
territorial / balance-of-power map layer.** The re-baseline's single loudest signal
is that same-faction competent play collapses into a *passive economy race* (S2
engagement down ~5–6×, 3/10 matches zero-combat) — the bots have no reason to seek
contact. The North Star behavior "always push where the enemy is comparatively weak;
the front steps forward wherever it is safe" is the direct antidote: it converts the
passive stalemate into a contact-seeking advance, which is precisely what the S2
metric (net combat swing) and its engagement floor are built to reward. I therefore
recommend **starting the territorial layer now — as its smallest behavior-bearing
slice (a balance-of-power offense bias that reads the existing `InfluenceMap`), not
a big-bang rewrite** — as cycle 1, then clearing two cheap debts (disable the
negative-credit dispersion doctrine; ship the queued early-packet tuning), adding a
second map rung to prove the new behavior generalizes, and only then deepening the
territorial layer into its full fog-respecting classification form. Mission
abstraction — the other standing structural option — is costed and **deferred**: its
own decision rule is not met (capture is at parity, not failing on lost-TECN-no-retry),
so it would spend 1–3 days buying retry/staging the current bars don't ask for, while
the territorial layer moves the bars that are actually stuck. Determinism (the other
foundation) has **already landed** (seeded `LocalRandom`, `World.cs:217-228`); the
remaining foundation, structured telemetry, ships *alongside* cycle 1 rather than as
its own cycle.

## 1. The next behavior lever (Q1)

**Recommendation: the territorial / balance-of-power map layer, delivered as its
smallest behavior-bearing slice first — a "push where the enemy is comparatively
weak, advance the front where safe" offense bias driven by `InfluenceMap`.**

**Why this and why first:**

1. **It is the genuinely new lever the data demands.** The re-baseline's core finding
   is that the *only* Exp/Stable delta (SR multiplier) is exhausted. Every remaining
   knob on the score-floating axes is the same class of tuning that just plateaued.
   The balance-of-power reading is a *new input* (relative friendly/enemy influence
   per grid cell) the offense has never consumed — not a re-weighting of the old ones.
2. **It attacks the loudest signal in the data at the bot level.** S2's engagement
   collapse (3/10 zero-combat, vol −5–6×) is currently framed as an *instrument* risk
   to be papered over with a batch-validity gate / forced-contact map. But it is also
   a *behavior* finding: the bots have no drive to press a weakening sector, so they
   sit and economy-race. A bias that advances the front toward comparatively weak
   enemy cells makes the bot *seek* contact where it holds the advantage — fixing the
   stalemate structurally instead of re-scoping the map around it.
3. **It is the user's stated North Star centerpiece** (`ai-realism.md` §1, 2026-07-20):
   safe/grayzone/enemy classification, push-where-weak, reinforce-weak, front steps
   forward, end state = forces spread along the whole defended line. Slice 1 is the
   on-ramp to all of it and reuses the substrate that was clearly built for it
   (`InfluenceMap`, `FrontlineOverlay` are literally "Stage A" of a doctrine roadmap
   per their headers).

**Metric it should move:** **S2 net swing** (`kills_cost − deaths_cost`) — pressing a
weak sector should win the trade — **and the S2 engagement floor** (contact actually
happens, lifting the ≥6/10-engaged batch-validity count and shrinking the zero-combat
seed set). **S1: non-regression** only (S1 offers no economy edge over Stable by
construction). Success criterion for the cycle: S2 relative swing edge turns positive
(toward the proposed +$1,000 bar) *without* dropping engagement — i.e. Exp wins by
fighting smarter, not by fighting less.

**Slice boundary (what is IN cycle 1 vs deferred):** IN = read the *already-computed*
friendly/enemy influence grids, derive a per-cell balance-of-power ratio, add a
`@experimental`-only, default-frozen scoring term to `PoiOffensiveBotModule` that
biases axis selection toward advancing the frontline into comparatively-weak cells;
ship it with lightweight territorial telemetry markers (see §5). OUT (deferred to
cycle 5) = the fog-respecting classification proper (own-half-safe prior,
intel-driven safe/grayzone/enemy reclassification, no-see-through-fog gating) and the
reinforce-weak reserve behavior. Slice 1 uses the AI's current map knowledge; the
fog-respecting intel layer is the L-effort second slice.

## 2. Structural vs incremental (Q2)

**This recommendation IS the structural option**, delivered in slices — not an
incremental patch. The radical-change mandate is satisfied by *starting the
territorial layer* rather than tuning the existing modules further. Justification for
the one genuinely incremental cycle in the sequence (early-packet tuning, cycle 3):
it is a **cheap complement** to the territorial advance (small packets spreading to
grab/hold ground is North Star §2, "spread out and capture fast in small groups"),
it is per-instance YAML with the `@stable` twin provably untouched, and it is
`PoiOffenseTest`-verifiable without a game — it is not the main thrust and is not
sold as one.

**The competing structural option — mission abstraction — is costed and loses now.**
It is fully designed (`plans/260720_mission_abstraction_costing.md`): step 1
(CaptureMission, ~1–1.5 days) buys retry-on-loss + committed follow-escort + stall
detection. But its own decision rule (§4.3 of that doc) fires **only if** capture is
still <6/10 with logs pointing at lost-TECN-no-retry (F-1) or escort desync (F-4) as
the residual mode. The re-baseline shows capture at **parity 6/10** and the known
residual is **production throughput** (busy-queue request drop,
`UnitBuilderBotModule.BuildUnit:155`), not retry. So mission step 1 would spend a
structural budget buying a capability the current bars do not ask for, while leaving
the stuck bars (S2 swing/engagement) untouched. **Structural-here loses to
structural-there:** the territorial layer is the structural move that moves the stuck
metric. Mission abstraction stays the roadmap-1 target and becomes the right vehicle
*when the territorial layer needs "assault this sector / garrison this captured
ground" as first-class, retryable missions* (cycle 5–6 horizon) — unify it *as* that
need arises (its step-2 rule), never as a standalone refactor.

## 3. Dispersion branch verdict (Q3)

**Verdict: RE-VERIFY under the new regime via a clean A/B, then DISABLE on
`@experimental` (do not retune, do not leave as-is).**

Facts: the dispersion doctrine (`CohesionSwitchEnabled: true`, spread-to-move /
mass-to-assault) is **already merged into `main` and promoted into `@stable`** (LADDER
PROMOTE note) — it is not a parked-and-inert branch; it is live in *both* bots. Its
only causal measurement (the S2 ON/OFF A/B, `runs/260720_s2_exp_vs_normal_n10.md`)
came back **negative**: median paired delta ON−OFF = −$1,500, engagement ~20% lower
ON, its sole win a single high-variance seed flip. And it **predates** the evac / heli
/ TECN-ferry changes, so even that negative read is stale.

Why disable, not retune: the measured *sign* is wrong, and retuning is polishing a
lever pointing the wrong way — expensive for a doctrine whose value proposition
(survivability under fires) the RTS-format S2 does not reward. Why re-verify first:
the negative read is pre-regime and pre-ferry; one cheap paired A/B on the new regime
confirms the sign before we act. Mechanism: toggle `CohesionSwitchEnabled: false` on
`@experimental` only, A/B vs the dispersion-ON Stable on the fixed seed set (paired,
cheap — determinism makes this a low-N clean comparison). If confirmed
neutral/negative (expected), **land it off on `@experimental` and flag `@stable` for
a corrective toggle at the next PROMOTE** so the frozen control stops carrying a
negative-credit behavior. Bonus: disabling dispersion *unblocks* the early-packet
tuning (cycle 3) — the tuning design notes dispersion interacts with packet
granularity (`plans/260721_earlygame_tuning.md` §c.5), and removing it first lets
packet size be measured cleanly. This is why cycle 2 (dispersion) precedes cycle 3
(packets).

## 4. S2 signal — forced-contact redesign vs keep-with-gate (Q4)

**Recommendation to the loop: KEEP S2 on the quiet same-faction Stable regime with
the batch-validity gate, and let cycle 1 (the territorial contact-seeking lever) be
the test of whether the quiet is a bot problem or a map problem — do NOT redesign S2
to a forced-contact map yet.**

Reasoning: the 3/10 zero-combat is real, but redesigning the instrument now would
*mask the exact behavior we want to fix*. If the bots learn to push weak sectors
(cycle 1) and engagement rises to ≥6/10 on the *same* quiet map, that is strong
evidence the territorial lever works — a forced-contact map would have hidden that
signal by manufacturing combat regardless. So: adopt the proposed **blocking
batch-validity gate (≥6/10 engaged, else the batch is a passive stalemate and the
swing number is not trusted)** as the guard, run cycle 1, and read the engaged-count
as a *primary* cycle-1 outcome. **Decision branch:** if cycle 1 lifts engagement
≥6/10 → the regime is fine, keep it. If cycle 1 improves swing but engagement stays
<6/10 → *then* add a forced-contact variant (central-cluster spawn / shorter clock /
`@rush` control) as an S2 sibling, because at that point the quiet is proven to be
map geometry, not bot passivity. Either way the user's open question resolves on
data from the very next cycle rather than on a guess now.

## 5. Foundations — determinism & telemetry (Q5)

- **Seeded `LocalRandom` determinism: DONE.** Landed at `World.cs:217-228` (seed
  derived from the lobby seed via a decorrelated LCG transform; same-seed→byte-identical
  verified, LADDER Metric-extraction note). Nothing to schedule. The re-baseline and
  every cycle below already run paired on the fixed seed set — this is the throughput
  + variance-reduction win that makes low-N A/B (cycles 2, 3) trustworthy.
- **Structured telemetry: ships ALONGSIDE cycle 1, not as its own cycle.** The
  territorial behavior is exactly the kind whose success is invisible in win/loss
  alone — you must be able to see "did the front advance? did it push the weak sector?
  did engagement rise *because* of the push?". Precedent: the capture-marker cycle's
  landed value was its F-5 observability, not the score move. So cycle 1 ships with a
  small set of `[exp-terr]` markers (frontline-advance cells, weak-sector-push axis
  open, engagement-on-push) in the same idiom as the existing `[exp-*]` log ecosystem
  and the `InfluenceMapMath.CountFrontlineCells` diagnostic already present
  (`InfluenceMap.cs:262`). This is the seed of roadmap item 6 (telemetry-driven
  diagnosis) and grows `parse-s2-batch.py` into the analyzer — but it is *bundled*
  with the lever that needs it, respecting one-behavior-per-cycle by treating the
  markers as instrumentation of that behavior, not a separate behavior.

**Ordering verdict:** determinism = already before everything (done); telemetry =
alongside cycle 1; no foundation blocks the sequence.

## 6. Ladder growth — Polar Disorder + Woodland Warfare (Q6)

**Recommendation: add the Polar Disorder rung AFTER cycle 1 shows a signal on River
Zeta (slot it as cycle 4), and hold Woodland Warfare until the fuller territorial
classification (cycle 5) needs it.**

Why after cycle 1, not before: you want the new lever validated on the *known*
battlefield first (River Zeta, where all the baselines live) before spending N on new
maps. But you want the second map *before* the fuller fog-respecting classification
(cycle 5), because a territorial/geometry-reading behavior is the single most
overfit-prone thing we could build on one map — River Zeta's specific derrick layout
and chokepoints could flatter a "push where weak" heuristic that fails on different
geometry. So the second rung is the anti-overfit gate on the territorial layer.

What each new rung measures that River Zeta cannot:
- **Polar Disorder** — different chokepoint/water geometry: tests whether the
  balance-of-power push generalizes when "advance the front" means funneling through
  constrained terrain rather than River Zeta's open middle. Discriminates a genuine
  weak-sector-seeking behavior from one tuned to River Zeta's specific lanes.
- **Woodland Warfare** — LOS/cover terrain: directly stresses the *fog-respecting
  intel* half of the territorial layer (cycle 5), where line-of-sight and cover change
  what the bot can actually observe and therefore how it classifies safe/grayzone.
  Slot it as the CALIBRATE + EXPAND for cycle 5, not before.

Each new rung is a standard EXPAND (CALIBRATE for side-fairness + min-engagement, then
a ratified bar) — cheap now that S1 runs ~70s/match.

## 8. Recommended sequence — the next ~5 cycles

1. **Cycle 1 — Territorial balance-of-power offense bias (NEW LEVER).** Read
   `InfluenceMap` friendly/enemy influence; bias `PoiOffensiveBotModule` to advance
   the front into comparatively-weak enemy cells. `@experimental`-only, default-frozen.
   Ships with `[exp-terr]` telemetry markers. *Verify:* S2 swing + engaged-count vs
   `@stable` (paired) — this run also gives the already-merged TECN ferry its first
   regime verdict. Target: S2 relative swing → positive **without** dropping engagement;
   engaged-count ≥6/10. S1 non-regression.
2. **Cycle 2 — Dispersion re-verify + disable.** Paired A/B (OFF vs ON-Stable) on the
   new regime; confirm negative/neutral; disable on `@experimental`; flag `@stable` for
   a corrective toggle at the next PROMOTE. Cheap; unblocks cycle 3.
3. **Cycle 3 — Early-packet granularity.** `UnitsPerAxis` 8→4–5, `MinAxisSize` 3→2,
   `MaxAxes` 4→5–6 on `@experimental`. Complements the advancing front (small packets,
   grab fast). `PoiOffenseTest` + S1 tempo / S2 non-regression.
4. **Cycle 4 — EXPAND: Polar Disorder rung.** CALIBRATE + ratified bar. Anti-overfit
   gate: does the cycle-1 territorial push generalize off River Zeta's geometry?
5. **Cycle 5 — Territorial classification layer (slice 2).** Fog-respecting
   safe/grayzone/enemy with the own-half-safe prior and intel-driven reclassification;
   capture+fortify safe ground. Validated across River Zeta + Polar Disorder; Woodland
   Warfare stands up here (CALIBRATE) to stress the LOS/intel half. This is the North
   Star centerpiece proper.

**Horizon (cycle 6+):** reinforce-weak / spread-along-front reserve behavior (ties the
balance-of-power layer to `LayeredDefence` reserves — the "every part of the front
defended, strength shifted to thin sectors" end state); mission abstraction step 1 as
the vehicle when assault/garrison need to become first-class retryable missions.

## 9. Ranked alternatives (if cycle 1 is rejected)

1. **Early-packet granularity first (cycle 3 promoted to cycle 1).** Cheapest, lowest
   risk, still North Star §2. Rejected as *first* because it is the same incremental
   class the re-baseline says has plateaued — a good complement, a weak headline.
2. **Mission abstraction step 1 first.** Fully designed, structural, buys real
   capability. Rejected because its decision rule is not met and it does not move the
   stuck S2 metric — it would be structural work aimed at a bar that is already at
   parity.
3. **S2 forced-contact redesign first.** Would restore combat signal immediately.
   Rejected because it masks the passivity that cycle 1 is meant to cure; held as the
   conditional fallback (§4).
4. **SR-multiplier sweep (260 vs higher/lower).** The obvious incremental next step on
   the existing delta. Rejected outright — this is exactly the tuning the re-baseline
   declared exhausted vs a competent Stable.

## 10. Risks & guards (carried into cycle 1)

- **Frozen-control drift.** Every cycle is `@experimental`-only with a default-frozen
  kill-switch; verify `@stable` byte-identical before merge (the `MissionModeEnabled` /
  `CohesionSwitchEnabled` pattern).
- **"Push where weak" degenerates into an empty-ground economy grab** (captures derricks,
  never fights). Guard: the bias must key on *advancing the frontline* (contested band
  moving forward), not merely "lowest enemy influence anywhere" — read the engaged-count
  as a first-class cycle-1 outcome, not just the swing.
- **MiniYaml blank-line merge** on every `ai.yaml` edit (project hard rule).
- **Overfit to the fixed 10 seeds / River Zeta** — cycle 4 (Polar Disorder) is the
  explicit anti-overfit gate before slice 2 deepens the layer.
- **TECN-ferry confound.** The ferry is an unmeasured `@experimental` delta already in
  the tree; cycle 1's verify batch measures Exp-with-ferry vs Stable, so a cycle-1
  regression could be ferry-caused. If cycle 1 reads worse than the re-baseline, A/B
  the ferry toggle before blaming the territorial term.
