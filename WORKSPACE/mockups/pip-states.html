Detectability pip — state schematic

Schematic, not final art. Every number and code reference below was read out of the tree at main @ fa284169. Figures are inline SVG at diagram scale — the shipped pip is a small sprite; these are blown up so the shapes can be argued about.

Five things read out of the code that reshape this

1. Orange already ships. It waits on nothing.

AutoTarget.cs:765-781: a unit in Ambush with a live target pre-aims at :765, asks var isSpotted = self.CanBeViewedByPlayer(targetOwner) at :769, and then — on the !stage3 branch, which is every human-controlled unit, since stage3 = AmbushTacticsGranted(self) (:720) is a bot-only condition —

if (isSpotted || ambushTriggered) { … Attack(…); }
return;

So he rotates onto the target, asks whether that enemy can see him, and returns without firing when the answer is no. That is the orange state, running in the shipped game today, with no prerequisite — and with no readout whatsoever. Only the movement halt is gated behind enable-ambush-tactics (AttackMoveActivity.cs:190-195); the fire veto, the commoner half, is not.

This is the single strongest argument for the whole feature. Orange is not a state to design and build. It is a state the player is already in, several times a minute, with nothing on screen to tell him.

2. Spotted and blocked are not a collision — they are a contradiction.

Being seen is the veto's exit condition. The line is literally if (isSpotted || ambushTriggered) → Attack. The moment red becomes true, orange stops being true by construction. A precedence scheme for "both at once" would be machinery for a case the code mostly forbids.

That inverts the reason for the conclusion without changing it. Not "they collide constantly, so they need separate channels" but "they barely collide — so give colour wholly to the detectability continuum and spend one cheap second channel on the rare veto."

3. Even so, colour cannot carry both — and the reason is sharper than a clash of hues.

The veto is live only while not spotted. So "blocked" occupies precisely the not-yet-red band — which is the entire territory of the graded yellow in §2. It is not that the ramp happens to pass through orange; it is that blocked lives inside the ramp for its whole duration. One channel cannot express a continuum and a bit that is defined over the same interval.

The good news is how cheap the fix is. The veto is one bit, off most of the time, so it needs a modifier and not a scale.

4. The cheapest second channel costs one character.

WithSpottedDecoration extends WithTextDecoration (:45) — the mark is text, not a sprite. So a hollow-centre modifier is a one-glyph substitution, ◆ → ◇, with no new art, no new sprite row and no extra footprint. Drawn as candidate H below.

Caveat carried honestly: §1's kite and §2's ramp assume a filled shape whose hue is the signal. A text glyph's colour is set per-decoration, so the ramp is still expressible — but the kite silhouette in these drawings is schematic. If the mark stays text, the real shape is whatever the font gives.

5. Two costs the pictures would otherwise hide.

A graded ramp will step, not glide. RecalculationInterval = 7 (:33) with the answer cached in between — at 16.67 tps that is a refresh roughly 2.4× per second. That is fine for a binary badge, which is what it was tuned for, and visibly chunky for a gradient meant to read as motion. If grading is adopted, that interval has to come down, and that cost belongs here rather than arriving as a surprise later.

The bottom of the scale is not "safe". The anti-wallhack rule (:20-22, :105-111) means an enemy who can see you but whom you have not spotted lights nothing. So an empty pip means "you know of no watcher" — never "you are unobserved". That is a lie the engine deliberately refuses to tell, and every label on this readout has to survive it.

Minor, but worth catching early: ^HoldingFireMarker (defaults.yaml:847-856) already draws pip-orange at Position: TopRight for a deliberately-set stance. An orange detectability pip at Top would put two orange pips on one soldier meaning different things. A naming collision to resolve, not a reason to change course.

1 · The three states, plus the baseline

A soldier with his health bar beneath him and the pip hovering above, centred. The pip is a kite — widest above its midpoint, tapering to a point underneath so it reads as belonging to the man below it rather than floating free.

2 · Yellow does not have to be flat

A flat yellow covers everything from "an enemy is somewhere out there" to "one more step and he has you." Those are different orders. The margin that separates them is already computed and discarded — VisionCovers (:136-157) weighs each observer's band strength against CurrentVisibility and its range against the actual distance, then throws both away at return true. Grading needs no new spatial query, only a different return type.

Nothing here computes closing speed, so "two seconds from being seen" is not free. What is free is how deep inside his envelope you already are.

Two costs, both real. The ramp occupies the whole not-spotted interval, which is exactly where the veto also lives — so the veto must be a modifier, never a hue (finding 3). And at RecalculationInterval = 7 the ramp updates about 2.4× a second, so it will step rather than glide unless that interval comes down (finding 5). If the ramp is adopted, red should also keep something categorical that no yellow ever has — the red pips on this page carry a heavier outline and wider glow for that reason, so "spotted" stays a step rather than just a shade.

Where they genuinely do coexist — two narrow cases

The contradiction is not total. Two situations put a real red and a real veto on the same soldier, and both are more drawable than the general case.

4 · What the pip would be reading — all of it already ships

Concealment in WW3MOD is an integer ladder of additive modifiers. Nothing below is proposed; it is all live.

Dug in (still ≥ 200 ticks ≈ 12 s)+1@Dugin · :774-776, granted by GrantConditionOnMovement · :139-142
Moving−1@Moving · :785-787
Firing any weapon−2@Firing · :782-784, held 12 ticks after the shot

So the orange state is not a new rule — it is a readout of arithmetic the player is already subject to and cannot currently see. A dug-in prone soldier is +2; the moment he fires he is 0, and stays there for 12 ticks. That swing is what the veto at AutoTarget.cs:774 is protecting, and the entire content of the orange state.

The prone cliff — why hunker depth must not simply grant prone

Prone protection ships as geometry, not as a damage modifier: HitShape@Cover swaps the hit circle from radius 30 to radius 20 (infantry.yaml:143-150). Drawn to scale:

That is a large, real, already-shipping protection term. It is also a cliff: wiring "hunker depth" as grant the prone condition takes the player from 100% to 44% hit area in one click, and leaves no gradient to tune afterwards.

Posture should be continuous, with prone as an interior anchor on the same axis — the radius already moves, so let depth move it smoothly and let 20 be a point on the curve rather than its only destination.

One more wrinkle before anything is wired: prone is not only a suppression effect. ProneCondition: deployed || suppressed > 30 || !moving || critical-damage (infantry.yaml:315) — !moving means standing still already puts a soldier prone, independent of suppression. Any hunker control arrives on top of an axis the player is unknowingly driving already.
