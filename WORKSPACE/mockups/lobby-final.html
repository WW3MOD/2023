The lobby, settled — two modes, and neither of them is complicated

Escalation is two sides, one setting: when the first warheads arrive. Skirmish is buy your own: nuclear weapons unlock for purchase on a schedule, nothing is granted, and firing one escalates nothing. Dead Hand is a time limit either mode can switch on. Tab 1 is the one to build; 2–4 are last ideas.

What the two modes now are, in one line each. Skirmish — the ordinary game. Nukes are a purchasable superweapon gated behind a clock, like any other RTS; using one has no consequence beyond the crater. Escalation — the exchange: every side is handed one small warhead at the interval, and firing arms the side you hit with the next yield up. Firing is a gift to your enemy, restraint is the only brake, and reaching the game-enders means everyone is recorded as Lost.

Why this is the one

  • Four new controls in total, across both modes: nuclear availability, highest yield, the unlock interval, and Dead Hand's length. Escalation adds exactly one. Everything else on screen either ships today or is read-only.
  • The read-only blocks do the teaching. Skirmish's schedule and Escalation's five lines are the only documentation either mode needs, and they sit where the decision is made rather than in a tooltip nobody opens.
  • The two-sides constraint is stated where it bites — in the panel, at pick time, saying what will happen to the current lobby rather than refusing at launch.
  • changed The four placeholder-dimmed dropdowns that ship today (Game Mode, Start At, Escalation Pace, Nuclear Ceiling) become Game Mode plus the two DEFCON fields. Pace and Ceiling are gone — pace was a DEFCON-3 clock nobody could interpret, and the ceiling only ever meant something in the old ladder.

The one thing I would watch

  • "Unlock a tier every 10 minutes" with a 100 kt cap means the last tier arrives at 40:00, which is most of a match. If Skirmish nukes are meant to be a mid-game tool rather than a late-game one, the interval wants to be 5–7 minutes, not 10. Worth one play test before the default is fixed.

The argument

  • The two-line pitch on each card is the best advertising either mode will ever get, and a dropdown gives it nowhere to live.
  • Seeing "two sides only" and "nothing purchasable" before picking is worth more than seeing them after.

Against

  • It costs vertical space in a lobby that also has ten player slots and a map preview, and the unselected column is permanently dead pixels.
  • Both columns have to stay honest as the modes change, which is two things to maintain instead of one.

The argument

  • This was too radical when there were twelve settings. With two it is close to honest — the timeline is the configuration now, not a visualisation of it.
  • A host can see the shape of the match before playing it: a long conventional stretch and a short nuclear one, or the reverse.

Against

  • Dragging needs a keyboard path and real interaction work for two values a dropdown does in a line of markup.
  • It implies the match has a length. It does not, unless Dead Hand is on.

The argument

  • "They escalate" as an answer to a question teaches the mode better than the word Escalation on a dropdown ever will.
  • The summary line is the whole configuration in one sentence, which is also exactly what should be posted to the chat when the host changes something.

Against

  • Question one silently merges two things — whether nukes exist and how they behave. That is elegant right up until a fourth mode exists.
  • Hiding DEFCON under Advanced buries the setting most likely to be argued about before a match.
