More than two sides — and the answer is that nothing fundamental changes

The short version of the whole page: your ladder with a side climbs when you damage that side. That one sentence is format-agnostic. It reduces to your 1v1 rule exactly, it handles teams, three-way games, uneven teams and free-for-alls, and "what if two players are hit at once" stops being a question rather than needing an answer.

You do not need to hold the scenarios in your head — the rule holds them for you. That is the test a rule has to pass: if it needs a special case per format, it is the wrong rule. This one needs none. What DOES change with player count is only how many numbers are on screen, which is a display problem and is tab 3.

Not "who was targeted" — who was hit. Let the blast decide.

Aim points are a poor witness: they land on neutral ground, on a unit that moved, on a seam. Damage is not. Every hostile side that actually took meaningful damage climbs one step, and the question of intent never has to be answered.

The rule, entire
· A side is a team, or a lone player in a free-for-all.
· Any hostile side taking at least 10% of a detonation's damage climbs one step on its ladder with you. Relative, so it never needs per-yield tuning. new
· Their new ceiling is one above the highest yield you have ever landed on them — not one per launch. Firing 1 kt five times escalates once.
· One warhead granted per step, to the player who took the most damage.
· Your own side and your allies are never armed by your launch, whatever they absorb.

Your two questions, answered

  • "How do we calculate who was targeted?" — Don't. Attribute by damage dealt, after the fact. It is robust against a missed aim point, a moved unit, and a MIRV whose six re-entry vehicles land on three different people.
  • "What if two are hit at the same time?" — Both climb. It is not a special case, which is the sign the rule is the right shape. And it makes the aim point a diplomatic act: drop one on the seam between two enemies and you have escalated with both, deliberately or by accident.

What falls out of it for free

  • new The demonstration shot. A warhead into empty ground hits nobody, so it escalates nothing. You have spent a nuke to prove you have one. That is a real Cold War move and nobody had to design it — it is just what the rule says.
  • It also means escalation is caused by killing people, not by owning weapons, which is the morally exact version and reads well in the log: "AMERICA HAS BEEN STRUCK" rather than "America has been targeted".

The one number to tune

  • The 10% floor. Too low and a stray point of splash on a scout drags a third party into a war; too high and a genuine glancing blow escalates nothing. It wants a play test, not an argument — but it is one number and it is easy to move.

The verdict you asked for
The fundamental mechanic does not need to change, and it does not need to adapt to the player list. One rule covers every row above. The only thing that scales with the lobby is how many ceilings are drawn, and that is tab 3.

Two consequences worth keeping deliberately

  • A teammate can drag you into a nuclear war. One player on your side fires; the enemy's ceiling now applies to all three of you. That is the truest thing in the design — it is how Article 5 works — and the answer is information, not a vote: announce it to your own side, and make the confirmation say what it will do to your allies.
  • Firing at the weakest enemy arms the weakest enemy. Choosing a victim is choosing whom you hand a bigger warhead to, so the cheap target and the safe target stop being the same target. That tension is worth more than it costs.

The only genuine fork — and in 1v1, 2v2 and 3v3 the two are identical.

It only matters with three or more sides on the map. Both are defensible; one is correct and one is cheap, and there is a way to have both.

Recommended
Per pair of sides
  • + Escalation is a relationship. Your ceiling with America says nothing about your ceiling with the third team.
  • + No collusion vector: two players cannot lightly nuke each other to arm one of them against a third.
  • + Nobody is ever surprised by a warhead from someone they never exchanged fire with.
  • − N² state. Six-way FFA is fifteen ladders.
  • − Needs a per-opponent readout on the player list.
Simpler
Per side
  • + One number per player. Trivial to draw, trivial to explain.
  • + Models proliferation: once anyone builds bigger, the whole world is more dangerous.
  • − Being attacked arms you against people who never touched you. Someone will feel cheated, and they will be right.
  • − Opens collusion: nuke your friend gently to arm them against the leader.
How to have both
Implement per pair — it is a dictionary keyed on a side-pair instead of on a side, and that is the whole difference in code.
Display per side until there are three. In 1v1, 2v2, 3v3 and 2v1 there is exactly one opponent side, so one number is the complete truth and the player list shows a single ceiling. The per-opponent column only appears in the formats that actually need it.
That way the complexity is paid for only by the players who chose a six-way free-for-all.

Doomsday and Escalation are two doors into the same room.

Making Doomsday an option for any match is right, and it makes the ending one implementation instead of two: whichever door you come through, everyone is armed and everyone gets fifteen seconds.

Door 1 · EscalationA player fires a game-ender. The decision is theirs, and it is the moral of the mode.
▶
The roomEvery surviving side is granted its enders. 15 s, centre screen, then aside. Click targets. All of it lands together.
◀
Door 2 · DoomsdayThe match clock expires. The decision is the host's, made in the lobby an hour earlier.

What this settles

  • changed Dead Hand's automatic salvo goes. Manual targeting replaces it in both doors, which is the better scene and deletes a whole code path.
  • new Doomsday becomes a setting, not a mode — "end this match after 1h" available anywhere, off by default in Escalation. Left off, an Escalation match still ends only when a player decides it does.
  • Skirmish keeps it exactly as today's time limit, with the twist at the end.

One thing manual targeting loses, and how to keep it

  • The automatic salvo guaranteed the map was destroyed. Manual does not: a player who is AFK, or dead, or who picks two targets out of three, leaves warheads unfired and the map partly standing — which undercuts the ending badly.
  • ships Keep the guarantee by reusing what is already there: when the fifteen seconds expire, every unspent warhead fires on its own, aimed by the existing DoomsdayStrike targeting — the cities-and-outliers pass that picks aim points today. Agency where a player wants it, apocalypse regardless.
