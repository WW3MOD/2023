Suppression readout — comparison sheet

Every number on this page is read out of the shipped rules, not invented. main @ 6a7e1839 · mods/ww3mod/rules/ingame/infantry.yaml

Two corrections to the brief I was given, found while reading the file. Both change the design conversation.

1. Prone is not inert — it halves your hit area. The brief said the five prone damage tiers almost never fire, which is true. But HitShape@Cover (infantry.yaml:143-146) shrinks the soldier's hit circle from radius 30 to radius 20 the moment he goes prone. That is an area ratio of 44% — a prone soldier is a 56% smaller target, and this fires against everything, not just warheads with a matching damage type. So a protection term tied to suppression already exists and already works. It arrives as geometry, not as a damage modifier, which is why a grep for damage multipliers missed it.

2. There is already a passive, time-based hunker. GrantConditionOnMovement (:139-141) grants dugin after TimeToBeStill: 200 — 200 ticks ≈ 12 seconds of holding position — and dugin buys +1 concealment tier (:774-776). So "stay still, become harder to see" ships today. The proposed hunker is not a new idea in this codebase; it is an existing idea with no readout and no player control.

2 · The two candidate treatments, head to head

A · Ten discrete pips — what ships today

WithDecoration@Suppression_1..10 draws one of ten pip-suppression-N images at Position: Top, RequiresSelection: true. Ten hues, one per band of 10.

4× inset
Reads severity, not trend. Band 7 looks identical whether it is climbing towards pinned or decaying back to safe — and those demand opposite orders. It also vanishes when you deselect, so you cannot watch a squad you are not clicking on. At 1× the ten steps are distinguishable by colour only; the width never changes, so peripheral vision gets nothing.

B · Continuous bar + trend arrow — proposed

One 24 px track. Fill length = suppression. Fill colour = severity. A one-character arrow on the right edge = sign of the last ~½ s of change. A hairline tick sits at 30, the prone threshold, so the player can see the line he is about to cross.

4× inset
Length carries severity even out of focus, so colour is reinforcement rather than the only channel — this also survives red-green colourblindness, which A does not. The arrow answers the actual question: is this getting worse? Cost: 3 px of vertical space and one more sprite row than A.
Honest legibility note at real size. At 1× the trend arrow in B is a 4×4 px glyph. On this page, on a desktop monitor, it is legible but marginal — and it would be the first thing to fail on a 4K screen at default UI scale. If B is chosen I would drop the arrow glyph and instead animate the trailing edge of the fill: a brighter 2 px cap that leads the bar when climbing and lags behind it when decaying. Same information, no glyph, and it reads at any resolution because it is motion rather than shape. Treatment C below shows it.

C · The same bar, trend shown by a leading edge instead of a glyph

4× inset · animated

Left: climbing — the bright cap runs ahead of the fill. Right: decaying — the cap trails behind it. Watch for two seconds; the difference is pre-attentive.

3 · What every band actually costs

Straight from the five ladders. Shots/min vs calm is derived — BurstMultiplier ÷ BurstWaitMultiplier — because those two stack and neither alone tells you the firing rate.

The single most important row is band 10. Speed 0, vision 0, burst 0. A pinned soldier cannot move, cannot see and cannot shoot — he is already contributing nothing. That is what makes the hunker proposal cheap at the top of the ladder: at band 10 there is no firepower left to trade away. The interactive explorer draws that as a curve; it is the crux of whether the system is worth building.
An asymmetry worth a decision. The speed ladder is gated !panicking && suppressed > N, but vision, burst, burst-wait and inaccuracy are not. So a panicking soldier drops the suppression speed penalty (panic sets its own speed) while keeping all four combat penalties. That is probably intentional — panic is a movement state — but it means "suppressed" is not one coherent state in the code, and any readout showing a single number is smoothing over a real seam. Flagging it, not fixing it.

4 · Where a hunker axis would attach

Today suppression is a single involuntary axis: it goes up when shot at, decays at 1 point per 5 ticks (≈ 30 s for a full 100 → 0 at 16.67 tps), and every one of its five effects is a penalty. The player's only input is leave.

The proposal adds a second, voluntary axis — hunker depth — that the player controls, which buys protection with firepower. The two existing pieces it should be built out of, rather than alongside:

  • Hit-circle radius — already 30 standing / 20 prone. Hunker depth continues that same shrink downwards. One mechanism, three points on it, no new damage system.
  • Concealment tier — already +1 prone, +1 dug-in, −2 while firing. Hunker adds tiers on the same integer ladder, and the existing grey range circle already renders it.

Built that way the "new" system is mostly exposing controls and a readout for machinery that is already in the build, which is a much smaller change than it sounds — and it matches the recorded preference for a gradient that prices a bad state rather than a mode that throws the player out of it.
