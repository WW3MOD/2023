Why this one

  • Two cards is the shortest possible sales pitch for a mode people have never heard of. The dropdown in tab 1 is functionally identical and advertises nothing.
  • The card copy does the teaching a tooltip currently has to: "the war goes nuclear on a clock, and ends" is the whole mode in nine words.

Against it

  • It reads as though there will one day be six cards. If more presets ever arrive, this is the layout that has to change.

Why this one

  • "Ends in ~48 min" is printed on the switch itself. No other layout here answers the question a host is really asking, and it costs one line.
  • The full schedule doubles as the mode's documentation. Nobody needs a tooltip explaining what Escalation is once they can see it.

Against it

  • Seven rows is the tallest of the three, and four of them never change.
  • The estimate is load-bearing and slightly dishonest — one early skirmish moves everything below it by minutes. It could be collapsed to just the last row ("ends ~47:30") if the table proves too much.

Not a lobby panel — this is what the mode is for, and it is the part with the most moving pieces. One player fires; everyone else gets fifteen seconds, their game-enders already selected, and all of it lands together.

What is already built

  • ships The weapon. RS-28 Sarmat MIRV — six 750 kt re-entry vehicles, and it already asks for six aim points, one warhead each. Clicking targets under a countdown is the interaction it was built for.
  • ships Score frozen at launch. Dead Hand already counts the score before impact, so whoever was ahead on the field wins. The ending is a match clock, not a coin flip — worth saying out loud, because it is what makes the mode fair.

What the fifteen seconds needs

  • new The banner, the countdown, the move-aside, and auto-selecting each player's ender so a click is a target and nothing else.
  • new Bots have to use it. A bot that ignores the window wastes its warheads and the ending becomes one-sided against a human. This is the piece most likely to be underestimated.
  • new Unspent seconds. If someone picks two of three targets and the clock runs out, the third should still fire — at their last target, or at the firer. Silently losing it feels like a bug.

The one hole in the rules as written

  • Nobody has to fire. The final step hands everyone a game-ender; if all sides sit on theirs, the match runs forever with the apocalypse in everyone's pocket.
  • Cheapest fix, no new setting: the final step starts one more interval, and when it expires the enders launch by themselves — everyone gets the same fifteen seconds. Firing early is then a real decision (you pick the moment) rather than the only way the match can ever end.
