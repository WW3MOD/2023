One floating diamond, two channels — fourteen ways to draw it

Every row below is the same mark in the same place: one diamond floating over the unit, at the shipped anchor (Position: Top, Margin 0,-10 on vehicles, 0,-16 on infantry). Nothing else is added anywhere on the unit. The rows differ only in how the diamond encodes its two channels — impediment and detection.

Every diamond here is rasterised at true pixel size in Python and shipped as a PNG (WORKSPACE/mockups/diamond_variants_assets.py), not drawn as vector art and scaled. That matters: the question this page exists to answer is how many levels of each channel survive at 6×9 px, and a vector diamond scaled to 4× would show steps that do not exist on the pixel grid. Zoom below is nearest-neighbour over these exact pixels. Judge at 1×.

Units are the shipped sprites at their real RenderSprites: Scale — an Abrams is 29×24 px, a rifleman is 7×8 px, which is smaller than the 6×9 mark standing over him. The ground is the real clear1.tem grass. Palette caveat, restated: the canonical temperat.pal lives inside a Blowfish-encrypted local.mix, so terrain renders through plains.pal as a proxy; true in-game ground is somewhat more olive than this. Since the question these marks must survive is contrast against ground, treat every contrast verdict here as provisional and settle it in a capture.

One channel is shipped code. The other is a document.

Detection is real and graded today. Detectable.CurrentVisibility is live and recomputed every tick; Detectability.Grade (DetectabilityGrade.cs:64-96) inverts concealment into exposure and cuts it into the five bands Concealed / Low / Moderate / High / Spotted. Those five grades and their five shipped colours are on screen in the game right now.
Impediment does not exist. It is specified in WORKSPACE/impediment-spec.md §1.1 as I = clamp(0,100, S + D) and not one line of it is built — the spec's own staging plan lists the engine work (a decay-exempt ExternalCondition source, plus a new GrantImpedimentFromHealth trait) as unstarted. Every impediment value on this page is a number I chose to illustrate an encoding.

The standing rule here is that you never draw a mark as a substitute for a mechanic that does not work. This page is not that — it is design exploration ahead of the mechanic — but the asymmetry must not be hidden: if any of these shipped before impediment does, the colour channel would be showing a quantity the simulation cannot supply. Detection alone is drawable today.

Four things the rendering settled that I had wrong before I drew it

These are corrections to my own captions, made after looking at assets/_diamond-ladders.png — every variant's two channel ladders at 1× and 6×. They apply across the whole page.
  1. Five detection grades do not fit four fill steps. Any variant with a 4-step fill must merge two of Concealed / Low / Moderate / High / Spotted, and which pair merges is a design decision nobody has taken. Only row D is honest about this, because it merges down to three deliberately. Everywhere else the merge is an accident of rounding.
  2. The fifth fill step never arrives, at any glyph size. I expected the 9×13 body in row E to buy it. It does not: the rows a fifth step would use are the tip rows, and tips are thin at every size — at 9×13 the top three rows are 1, 3 and 5 px, 9 px out of 69. What the bigger glyph actually bought was a fifth colour band. The two channels have opposite appetites: fill is bounded by the silhouette and does not improve with area; hue is bounded by ink area and does.
  3. "Hollow" has less range than "full". Every mark needs a 1 px dark keyline to survive textured ground, and on a 34 px glyph the ring plus keyline are most of the ink — so an empty diamond reads as a dark blob with a coloured rim, not as an empty vessel. The bottom of the fill ladder is compressed on every row here.
  4. At 6×9 my diamond does not read as a diamond. Drawn as a solid shape with a keyline it is a rounded octagon; row E's 9×13 body is the only one with an unmistakable diamond silhouette. ⚠ This may be my rasteriser rather than the size. The shipped mark is the font glyph ◊/♦, whose tips are thinner than anything a filled shape can have, and FreeType's hinting is not reproduced here. It is the one shape claim on this page I would not defend without a capture.

Every variant, and the number that decides it

Claimed is fill steps × colour bands — what the encoding is authored to say. Resolves is my estimate of what a player can actually tell apart at 1× on textured ground. The gap between those two columns is the entire subject: the failure this whole strand exists to correct is a ten-step quantity drawn with three distinguishable states.

What "the diamond only" displaces — open, not decided

Fourteen other things are drawn over a unit today (WORKSPACE/indicator-audit.md §1). A single-glyph ruling does not fold them in — it evicts them, and that is a decision for the user, not for this page. None of the rows above draw any of these, which is what makes each row a fair test of the diamond alone and an unfair picture of the finished screen.
