1. The lobby, reorganised— grouped by the question the host is answering

The reason the current lobby feels incoherent is structural: it is grouped by which C# trait owns each option, not by what the host is deciding. The Doomsday Clock sits under Match because it is the time-limit trait; the four nuclear checkboxes sit under Support powers because they are the powers trait. Regrouped by decision, it falls into four blocks.

What moved, and why

The Doomsday Clock dropdown is gone. It was a time limit wearing a nuclear label; under your design the clock is the DEFCON ladder, and the ending it promised is the top of the nuclear one. Two controls that had to agree became one.

The four nuclear checkboxes are gone from this draw — that is fork C below, and it is the one I am least sure about.

Sandbox stops being a checkbox layered over a mode and becomes a mode. It is the one option whose whole purpose is to ignore every rule the others set up, so it should not be combinable with them.

Conventional needs no entry. "Start at DEFCON 1" already is it — open war, no build-up, nuclear ladder at its floor. That falls out of your design for free.

Still here, unchanged

Fog, Explored Map, Separate Team Spawns, Crates, Game Speed and Starting Units are untouched — they are not escalation and should not move.

Your twist, drawn literally: using a nuclear weapon pushes the ladder up faster than time alone. Reaching for the small ones is what unlocks the big ones.

The one thing I would change about your rungs

You said 4 and 3 were "probably something similar, not sure". Three rungs of not fighting is a long time to look at a river. The draw gives 4 indirect fire across a closed line and 3 the line opening — so every rung visibly changes something, and the tension builds instead of pausing. If that is wrong, the levers are the thing to redistribute, not the rung count.

Why 2→1 is an action, not a clock

It is the only transition in your description caused by a player. That makes DEFCON 1 feel earned rather than scheduled — and it means a cautious lobby can sit at 2 for a long time, which is a real strategic choice rather than dead air.

What "pressure" is

A single counter driving ladder 2 upward: a slow constant from time, plus a large jump per detonation. One number, so the UI can show it as a bar and a player can feel it move when they fire.

3. Three forks I cannot settle for you

Each changes the lobby drawn above. The panel shows my pick; the alternative is what you would get instead.

A. Is the nuclear ladder shared, or per player? drawn as: shared

Shared ← my recommendation

One rung for the whole match. Your first 1 kt arms the enemy's 20 kt.
  • This is the dilemma. Every nuclear decision is also a decision about what you hand the other side.
  • Doctrinally right — escalation is a property of the exchange.
  • You can be dragged up the ladder by someone else's choices.

Per player

Each player climbs their own ladder by their own use.
  • Fairer; nobody is punished for an opponent's aggression.
  • Removes the dilemma entirely — using a small nuke costs you nothing, so the correct play is always to use one.
  • Two ladders on screen instead of one.
B. Is the DEFCON 5 line a hard wall, or a penalty? drawn as: hard wall

Hard wall ← drawn

Units physically cannot cross. Authored per-map, following terrain.
  • Unambiguous, and it makes the build-up phase real.
  • This is the expensive part of the whole feature — a front line authored for each of ten maps, plus a runtime-mutable blocking layer that touches pathfinding and the nav-guard baseline.

Soft penalty

Crossing is possible but punished — no support, attrition, or an instant DEFCON drop.
  • Far cheaper: no authored geometry, no pathfinding work.
  • "Crossing the line drops DEFCON" is itself a strong mechanic.
  • Fuzzier. A player can simply buy their way past the rules.
C. Do the four nuclear checkboxes survive? drawn as: gone

Gone — the ladder decides ← drawn

Tactical / Strategic / Nuclear Arsenal / Sandbox fold into the mode and the ladder.
  • One place decides what is available. The mod's own world.yaml already argues this: "a mode selected by two dropdowns that must agree is a worse lobby than one."
  • Loses the per-weapon veto — "tactical yes, strategic never" becomes unsayable.

Kept, as a veto over the ladder

The ladder releases a rung; an unticked weapon still never appears.
  • Hosts keep fine control, and nothing that ships today is taken away.
  • Two systems that must agree, which is the thing the codebase already warned against.
  • A checkbox changes meaning from "available" to "may be released later".
