Read the break-even mark sceptically. The two curves are not really in the same currency — one is firepower, the other is survival — so putting them on one axis assumes 1 point of damage avoided is worth 1 point of firepower given up. That exchange rate is a design judgement, not something the code tells us. Change it and the mark slides. The shapes are the trustworthy part: red is flat at the top of the ladder, green never is.

The proposed model, in full — argue with these five lines.
Everything geometric hangs off one continuous posture axis: standing → prone → deep. Suppression > 30 forces posture to at least prone (shipped behaviour); hunker depth pushes it further. Built that way, no input step produces an output step.
· Hit radius 30 standing → 20 prone (both shipped today) → 14 at full depth, interpolated. Damage scales with area: 100% → 44% → 22%.
· Speed follows the same posture curve — 100% standing, exactly the shipped 60% at prone, 0 at full depth — then the band penalty on top.
· Firepower ×(1 − depth): reaches zero at 100. "They must emerge to fire."
· Suppression intake ×(1 − 0.7·depth): a deep-hunkered man accrues suppression at 30% rate. This is the term that actually breaks a pin.
· Vision ×(1 − 0.6·depth), and +1 concealment tier past depth 50, +2 past 85 — same integer ladder as prone and dug-in. Concealment is the one term that does step, because the engine's Detectable tiers are genuinely integers.
