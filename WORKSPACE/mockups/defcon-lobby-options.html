DEFCON in the lobby — three ways it could land

Same rules in all three, different framing. The question is not which looks best but what a host has to understand before they can start a game. What ships today is drawn first so the delta is visible. Nothing here is built, and every label below is read from the current world.yaml / PowersLobbyOptions.cs rather than invented.

"Nuclear apocalypse when the clock runs out"

The problem to solve
  • Four nuclear toggles, three different defaults, no visible ordering. Nothing says a 20 kt strike is a smaller step than a 6 Mt one.
  • The Doomsday Clock is a time limit, sitting in a different section from every nuclear option, though it is the most nuclear thing in the lobby.
  • A host who wants escalation cannot express it — only pre-authorise each weapon before anyone has done anything.
What DEFCON adds
  • A wall clock 5→1 that ordinary fighting does not touch, releasing powers as it falls.
  • A separate annihilation counter moved only by nuclear use.

The ladder is shown, not edited. Pace scales every step together.

Why this one
  • The mod already made this argument. world.yaml says of the Doomsday checkbox: "a mode selected by two dropdowns that must agree is a worse lobby than one". This applies that to all six controls.
  • The host reads the ladder and knows what the match will feel like before starting. No other layout teaches the mode.
  • Conventional and Sandbox stop being a combination you must get right and become a thing you pick.
What it costs
  • Loses the per-weapon veto. A host wanting tactical nukes but never strategic ones can no longer say so.
  • Biggest change to what ships — the four checkboxes stop existing as lobby concepts, and every condition reading them needs rework.

Unticking a weapon removes it from the ladder; the rung still passes.

Why this one
  • Nothing that ships today is taken away and every existing condition keeps its meaning. Cheapest to build, least likely to break a scenario or a saved session.
  • Keeps the per-weapon veto — "tactical yes, strategic no" stays expressible.
What it costs
  • Two controls that must agree. Sandbox ON with DEFCON ON is a state somebody will pick, and it has to mean something.
  • A checkbox now means "may be released later" rather than "available" — the same word doing a different job.
  • The ladder is invisible. The host cannot see the pace they chose.

Two clocks, drawn apart on purpose: the upper one is time, the lower one is what players did.

Why this one
  • The only layout that shows the two-phase design honestly — a wall clock fighting cannot touch, and a counter only nuclear use moves.
  • A host can build the match they want: a fast conventional game that never reaches DEFCON 2, or a straight-to-1 knife fight.
What it costs
  • By far the most lobby surface, and OpenRA's lobby has no widget like this — new UI, not new options.
  • Every rung editable means every rung can be set to nonsense, and the lobby has to refuse it.
  • Overkill if most hosts only ever pick a preset.

What input would help most

1. Is DEFCON a mode, or a modifier? A and C treat it as the thing you pick instead of Conventional. B treats it as a schedule layered over powers you already chose. Everything else follows from this.
2. Do the four nuclear checkboxes survive? They are the host's per-weapon veto today. Under A they are gone; under B they change meaning to "may be released later". This was one of the three DEFCON questions left open.
3. Fixed ladder or authored ladder? Fixed 5→4→3→2→1 with one pace setting is a dropdown. An editable ladder is a panel. The build-cost gap is large; so is the gap in what a host can express.
4. What does DEFCON 1 actually do? Drawn here as "Dead Hand armed", folding the existing Doomsday Clock into the bottom rung. The alternative: DEFCON 1 only unlocks the last weapons, and the doomsday ending stays its own choice.
5. Sandbox — inside the ladder or outside it? An override in B, a peer mode in A. It is the one option that exists to ignore every rule the others set up.
