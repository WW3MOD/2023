Direction B — “Scoreboard”

Same flat table, but it answers who is winning and why instead of only who is here. Faction text is deleted (the flag already says it) and the 114px it was spending is repaid as four numeric columns. Rows carry a 3px army-share bar so the standings read at a glance without parsing digits.

Cost: YAML for the new columns + ~70 lines in GameInfoStatsLogic.cs to bind them. Every number already exists on PlayerStatistics (ArmyValue, UnitsKilled, DeathsCost, Experience — PlayerStatistics.cs:34-55) and the trait is attached to every player at mods/ww3mod/rules/player.yaml:204. The share bar is a ColorBlock with a computed Bounds.Width — no new widget type.

The one thing B gets right that A does not: a spectator and a mid-match player must not see the same table. Army value, kill counts and cash are intelligence. This mod has spent real effort on fog discipline; a scoreboard that leaks enemy army value through the Esc menu would undo it. Frame 2 shows the player view with enemy intel columns redacted to —.

Why these five columns and not others

Army PlayerStatistics.ArmyValue (:54). The single best “who is winning” number in an RTS, and the one the current table conspicuously lacks.
Kills / Lost UnitsKilled / UnitsDead (:48-49). Together with Army they separate ahead because they fought well from ahead because nobody attacked them — the exact question when watching six bots.
Cash PlayerResources.Cash. In a mod where units are called in against a budget rather than built, banked cash is unspent combat power, not idle savings.
Score PlayerStatistics.Experience — kept, unchanged, still the sort key.
Rejected APM (OrderCount) — meaningless for bots and near meaningless for one human. Harvesters / Oil Derricks — the observer Economy tab already carries them and they are not decisive here. Ping — not reachable; Session.Client exposes ConnectionQuality, not a latency figure.
