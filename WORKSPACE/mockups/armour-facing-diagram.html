Per-facing armour in the production tooltip — four options

Mockup only; nothing here is implemented. Base main @ 925b5b82. Every armour number shown is read from shipped YAML, not invented — see the findings below for file:line. Tooltip geometry is authored at true game pixels and scaled by the zoom control, so the heights quoted are the real cost.

First: what the engine actually supports

All five facings you named are real, and all five are authored today.

ArmorInfo.Distribution is a five-element array — { Front, Side, Rear, Top, Bottom } — declared at engine/OpenRA.Mods.Common/Traits/Armor.cs:31-32. It is consumed by DamageWarhead.ArmorDirectionPercent (Warheads/DamageWarhead.cs:142-219). Top and bottom are genuinely distinct facings, not an approximation. A weapon with TopAttack: true takes index [3] (:152-155); BottomAttack: true takes index [4] (:156-159). Your hedge — "and even top and bottom … I think" — was right on both counts.

Two corrections to how you described it, both of which matter for the diagram:

  • Left and right are not separate. The array has five slots but only four distinct values: index [1] is read for both the left and the right flank (DamageWarhead.cs:210-211 — leftDamage and rightDamage both multiply by distribution[1]). So it is front / side / rear / roof / belly, and a diagram must print one side number, mirrored, not two.
  • The values are percentages, not millimetres. Distribution entries are percent of Thickness. The engine computes effectiveThickness = thickness * armorPercent / 100 (DamageWarhead.cs:249, integer division) and compares that against the warhead's Penetration. So the per-facing millimetre figures a diagram would show are derived, not authored — a real quantity, but one that does not exist as a number anywhere in the YAML.

Is "mm" a fiction?

No, but it is a loose one. Thickness is declared [Desc("Armor thickness in mm.")] at Armor.cs:28-29, and the scale is internally coherent across the roster — aircraft skin 3–20, an APC 10–19, an MBT 280–700, a hardened bunker 2000. It is compared directly against warhead Penetration, which is the same scale. So the unit is the engine's own and the tooltip is not making it up. Whether individual values match reality is a balance question and separate: the T-90's 280 is well under commonly-cited real frontal figures.

The catch: only two actors in the whole mod have an interesting profile.

Sixteen vehicles author Distribution. Thirteen of them use the identical flat line 100,80,80,80,60 — front/side/rear/roof all within 20% of each other. Only abrams (100,40,15,10,10, vehicles-america.yaml:500) and t90 (100,60,40,15,15, vehicles-russia.yaml:322) are differentiated. On a Humvee the diagram would read 10 / 8 / 8 / 8 / 6 — four near-identical numbers and a shape that says nothing. Pick the Humvee in the control below to see how weak that case looks.

And the diagram immediately exposes a live authoring bug: the T-72.

t72 (vehicles-ukraine.yaml:25-26) is an MBT with Thickness: 280 — identical to the T-90 — but it carries the generic APC line 100,80,80,80,60. Its roof is therefore 224mm against the T-90's 42mm, 5.3× thicker, and its side is 224 against 168. That is almost certainly a copy-paste of the boilerplate onto a tank rather than a deliberate choice.

It has a real balance consequence today: the Javelin/ATGM is TopAttack: true with Penetration: 100 (weapons-missiles.yaml:6). Against a T-90 roof of 42 it penetrates and deals full damage. Against a T-72 roof of 224 it does not — ApplyPenetration (DamageWarhead.cs:128-133) scales damage to 100/224, about 45%. A top-attack missile is less than half as effective against the cheaper, weaker tank. I have not fixed this — it is outside this mockup's scope and it is a balance call, not a typo I should make unilaterally. Logged to WORKSPACE/bugs/discovered.md.

This is the strongest argument for building the feature at all: the bug has been sitting in the YAML unnoticed, and any of these four options would have made it visible the first time someone hovered a T-72.

Scope: vehicles only

Every Distribution in the mod is in vehicles*.yaml. Aircraft, structures and defences author Thickness but no Distribution — and ArmorDirectionPercent returns a flat 100% unless distribution.Length == 5 (DamageWarhead.cs:150), so their armour genuinely is uniform. The diagram must fall back to today's single-value row for them, which every option below does.

One nuance a four-number diagram slightly overstates

The horizontal facings are not four discrete zones. ArmorDirectionPercent linearly interpolates between neighbours by impact angle (DamageWarhead.cs:177-214): a hit exactly on the nose gets 100% of the front value, a hit at 45° gets a half-and-half blend of front and side. The authored numbers are exact at the four cardinals and smooth between them. Roof and belly are the exception — they are hard switches on the weapon flag, with no blending at all.

Your notes

Constraints I worked inside

  • The content column is exactly 350px, always. MaxTooltipWidth = 350 (ProductionTooltipLogic.cs:63) and leftWidth is Math.Clamp(…, MaxTooltipWidth, MaxTooltipWidth) (:165-167) — a clamp with equal bounds, so it is a constant regardless of content. Width is free; height is the only contested axis. Every option below exploits that by laying out horizontally.
  • Absolute pixel layout. LayOutElements stacks rows by incrementing y (:244-305). A diagram is a new TooltipElementKind that returns its own height — structurally the same as the existing Separator case, which already draws a ColorBlockWidget at an explicit WidgetBounds (:254-261).
  • Another worker owns the ammo and refill rows (wt/tooltip-ammo). The TANK ROUND … / AMMO / REFILL band and the 12px SectionGap above ARMOUR are drawn here as they stand at 925b5b82 and may move under them. I changed nothing there.
  • No new colours and no new sprite art in any option — A's silhouette is inline vector, not a unit sprite, so it needs no per-actor asset and cannot go stale when art changes.

Honest assessment

If you want my read: C is the one I would ship, and B is the one to pick if C feels too plain. The reason is the flat-distribution finding above — for 13 of 16 vehicles a diagram draws a large, careful picture of four numbers that are nearly the same, which is a lot of tooltip real estate spent conveying "this vehicle is not directional". C degrades gracefully there — it reads Light — 10 / 8 / 8mm over a legend line and costs 12px; A and D still draw the full picture for 74px and 57px respectively.

But that argument inverts if you intend to differentiate the rest of the roster. If every APC and SPG gets a hand-authored profile, the flat case disappears and A becomes worth its 74px. That is a content decision, not a UI one, and it is yours — so I have built all four rather than pre-empting it.
