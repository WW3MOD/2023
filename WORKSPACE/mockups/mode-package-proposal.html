Every mode serves one purpose, and they compose

A package, not a patch — proposed 2026-09-10 against the mode audit at WORKSPACE/reports/mode-audit-260910.md.

What is wrong, stated once

The lobby offers five mode-shaped controls that overlap and contradict each other. Two different features are named Sandbox. Doomsday cannot fire at its own defaults. The Sandbox game mode does nothing at all — except that at Start At 2 it freezes autonomous fire for the entire match, from a dropdown labelled "not yet implemented".

The deeper problem is the one the user named: Doomsday and Escalation feel like rivals because both are sold as "modes", when they answer different questions. Escalation governs what you may do, and when. Doomsday governs how the match ends. Sold as alternatives, they compete; sold as separate axes, they compose.

The proposal: three axes, each answering one host question

AxisThe question it answersChoices
1 · Escalation Do the rules of engagement change during the match? Off (default) — everything permitted from the first tick.
DEFCON — the three-level ladder: closed border, then weapons-free-by-order, then open war, with nuclear release gated behind it.
Carries Start At, Pace, and the Nuclear Ceiling.
2 · Ending How and when does the match stop? Last side standing (default) — the Supply Route decides it, as now.
Final Exchange after N minutes — the clock runs, and when it expires the automatic salvo levels the map; highest score at that instant wins.
3 · Arsenal Which weapons exist in this match at all? Tactical Nuclear Strike · Strategic Nuclear Strike · Nuclear Arsenal · Unlock all support powers (testing)

Every existing control lands on exactly one axis. Nothing new is invented except the merge of Doomsday and its clock into a single Ending control.

How the two axes combine — all four cells are real games

Ending: Last side standingEnding: Final Exchange
Escalation: Off The classic match. Contest the enemy Supply Route until they cannot be relieved. A timed brawl under a countdown. Build the biggest position you can — when the clock runs out the map dies and the score standing at that instant wins.
Escalation: DEFCON The war widens on its own terms. A closed border, then a phase where every shot is one you ordered, then open war and the nuclear ladder — and it ends when someone breaks. The full fantasy. Two clocks running against each other: how far up the ladder can you get before the deadline levels everything anyway.

The interaction that needs stating out loud

With both on, the Final Exchange fires regardless of which rung the ladder reached — even at HOLD, where no player may fire a warhead at all. That is deliberate and it is the point: the salvo is a Dead Hand, not a player order, so it is not gated by a release ladder that governs what players are permitted. A ladder that could suppress it would turn the deadline into a negotiation.

Where I would expect disagreement. Cutting the Doomsday checkbox removes a control that currently ships ON, so hosts who never open the options see a different default match: no time limit, no salvo, ending on the Supply Route. That is a real change in what the game is by default, and it is the one thing here worth arguing about — it can be reversed by shipping the Ending control defaulted to a duration instead.

What this does not touch

  • Supply Route contestation — the actual win condition, and the one system in the audit that surfaces itself properly. It is what "Last side standing" means.
  • The three arsenal checkboxes — they work, they are tested, and they are orthogonal to both axes.
  • The DEFCON internals — readout, border, hold-fire, five-rung ladder all shipped today and are unaffected by renaming the control that selects them.
