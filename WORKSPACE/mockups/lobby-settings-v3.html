Why this order

Match first because it decides what the other blocks even mean. Nuclear second because it is the mod's identity and currently the most buried. Economy and Battlefield are the ones a host changes rarely and reads often. Developer last, where it belongs.

Two controls replace five

Tactical Nuclear Strike, Strategic Nuclear Strike, Nuclear Arsenal, Sandbox and the Doomsday Clock all disappear. Nuclear Release and Ceiling say the same things in a way you can read in one pass.

Doomsday Clock is gone entirely

It was a time limit wearing a nuclear label. Under your design the clock is the ladder and the ending it promised is the top of the nuclear one, so keeping both would be two controls that must agree.

Sandbox is a mode, not a checkbox

Its whole purpose is to ignore every rule the other settings set up, so it should not be combinable with them. It is where the Tsar Bomba lives.

Skirmish needs no separate entry either

Game Mode → Skirmish is the old game: no ladder, no escalation, conventional only.

What changed from your first sketch

Five rungs became three. 5 and 4 are gone because you start at 3 — real-world standing posture — and there is nothing above it to climb down from.

DEFCON 2 is the interesting one

"Free to strike but only by direct order" is a mechanic I have not seen in an RTS. It makes the opening exchange deliberate: no unit shoots because it happened to see something, so the first casualty is always someone's decision. That it then immediately ends the phase is what gives it weight.

Two questions it raises

Does "one life" include a unit lost to something other than enemy fire — a crash, a crush, friendly fire? And does an enemy structure destroyed at DEFCON 2 count, or only a unit? Both change how fragile the phase feels.

Tsar Bomba

Kept in code, disabled in play, reachable in Sandbox. Nothing about the ceiling setting can reach it, which is what makes "sandbox only" true rather than merely default.

What I still need from you

1. The DEFCON 2 casualty rule. Does a non-combat death trip it? Does a structure count? This is the only rule in the design where I cannot guess your intent.

2. What "shared" means when a player fires. You said the mechanics need discussing. The simplest version: one pressure counter, every detonation adds to it, both sides read the same rung. The question is whether firing should push you up faster than your opponent — which would make going first costly rather than free.

3. Where the dividing line comes from. Hard wall is settled, but the line itself is authored per-map data. Ten shipped maps need one each, following terrain where it exists. That is the single largest piece of work in the whole feature and it has not started.
