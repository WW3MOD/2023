Anatomy of a Nuclear Detonation — WW3MOD

Every shipped number a player can perceive when a nuke goes off, read out of the files at main @ 3d1251a4 (3d1251a4d428bf750f19d87bd5c8943a30ed2782), 2026-09-07. Nothing on this page is what a number ought to be: where a value is derived by a law, the law is printed beside it and the shipped value is what is tabulated. Timestep is 60 ms → 16.667 ticks per second. In-tree comments asserting 25 tps are a known catalogued error and are not used here.

How to read this. Every row carries the YAML key that sets it. Point at a row; the key is the thing to change. Distances are in the mod's blast scale of 160 m per cell — deliberately NOT the weapon-range scale (a fourth-root compression, cells ≈ 18·km^0.23). Nothing on this page may be used to set a weapon Range. 1c0 = 1024 WDist units = one cell.

0 · The five laws every number falls out of

Blast radius — R_cells = 10 · Y[kt]^(1/3) · P[psi]^−0.589  (cube-root / Hopkinson-Cranz on the Glasstone airburst table, at 160 m/cell)

Fireball — radius r = 111 · (kt/20)^0.40 m  ·   duration D = 0.8 · (kt/20)^0.44 s

Thermal radius — R_cells = 2.0 · (kt/20)^0.41 km / 0.160  (grows as Y^0.41 against blast's Y^0.33, so the two cross at ~2.4 Mt: below that a weapon breaks further than it burns, above it the order inverts)

Shock birth — M = √(1 + (6/7)·ΔP/14.7) at the fireball surface, excess above Mach 1 falling linearly in radius to zero at TransitionRadius = 2 × StartRadius

Flash envelope — I(t) = 7.0 · (1 − t/D)², peak at tick 0, D = the fireball duration above. This one law is the whole of Observation A.

Sources: header of weapons-nuclear-arsenal.yaml:1–120 and the note on Atomic at weapons-superweapons.yaml:28–120. Every keyframe in §4 below was checked against the flash law and reproduces it to the printed decimal — the law is live, not aspirational.

2 · Damage

2.1 The three damage warheads, and which one does the killing

Every warhead here is the same Damage: 400000 and the same 35-point falloff shape across the whole arsenal (Atomic alone is Damage: 100000 with a 16-point table — it is the older, separately-balanced weapon). That is deliberate: cube-root scaling is the statement that peak overpressure at a given fraction of the blast radius does not depend on yield, so the yield lives entirely in the radii and the timings. Putting it into the damage number as well would count it twice.

2.2 What a unit actually takes — blast wave, one hit

A T-90 is HP: 24000, Armor: Heavy, thickness 280 (vehicles-russia.yaml:330). A rifleman is HP: 200, Armor: Kevlar (infantry.yaml:35,176).

Note the asymmetry, because it is not obvious from the YAML. The blast wave's Versus table lists only Light / Medium / Heavy / Concrete. Infantry armour types (Kevlar, None) are not in it, and DamageWarhead.DamageVersus returns a flat 100 when no listed armour type matches. So a T-90 takes 40% of blast damage and a rifleman takes 100%. Infantry also set no Thickness, so penetration never reduces it either.

3 · Shockwave — the two-phase blast front

The wave is born at the fireball surface, not at a point: inside the fireball there is nothing left to propagate. It leaves at its own breakaway Mach number and its excess above Mach 1 falls linearly in radius to zero at TransitionRadius; past that it holds WaveSpeed forever. Breakaway Mach falls with yield — 6.97 at 0.3 kt against 3.63 at 50 Mt — because a bigger fireball breaks away at a lower overpressure. That looks backwards and is correct.

4 · Light — the flash envelope (Observation A lives here)

The fireball is drawn twice, and the two draws are different objects:

  • Warhead@Fireball (CreateEffect + nuke_large) is the mushroom cloud — a sprite, tens of cells across, 199 ticks (12.0 s) long on every weapon.
  • Warhead@FireballLight (LightEvent) is the fireball and the flash it throws on the world — real illumination, tinting terrain and sprites. This is the one the user is asking about.
The envelope, in one line: I(t) = 7.0 · (1 − t/D)²  —  peak at tick 0, monotone decay, zero at D. Peak 7.0 is yield-independent on every nuclear weapon in the mod and that is deliberate: fireball surface brightness is set by temperature (~7000 K), so what yield buys is area and duration, not brightness. The envelope is single-peaked on purpose — a real fireball double-flashes, but at a 60 ms timestep bright–dim–bright is a strobe, and it was reported as one. Do not reintroduce a dip. Intensity 1.0 with Blend: Additive adds one unit of full-brightness light at the centre, i.e. doubles an already fully-lit sprite; 7.0 blows out hard toward white.

9 · Observation A — "the light is short-lived; the big ones fade well, the medium ones should too"

Quantified. Every flash envelope in the mod is the same curve, I(t) = 7.0·(1−t/D)², with the same peak of 7.0 at tick 0. There is no shape difference at all between a 20 kt flash and a 6 Mt one. The only thing that differs is D, and D is derived from yield by D = 0.8·(Y/20)^0.44 seconds — the fireball's own incandescent life. So "the large ones fade slower" is literally and only "the large ones have a bigger D".

9.1 The one number that governs it

Times: — specifically its LAST entry, inside Warhead@FireballLight → Light:. LightEventDefinition.Duration is defined as Times[Times.Length − 1] (LightEventDefinition.cs:172) and the light is removed the tick it is reached. Everything else about the fade — the squared decay, the colour ramp, the shrinking radius — is baked into the Intensities and Radii arrays as samples of that law, so changing D means re-evaluating those arrays at the new D. There is no exponent field and no decay-rate field; the curve is the numbers.

9.3 PROPOSED Giving the medium weapons the large weapon's decay character

Not applied. Nothing in the repo was changed. This section is a proposal to point at.

Two ways to do it, and they differ in what they cost the ends of the range:

Option 1 — an additive afterglow (recommended). Give the light its own duration law instead of borrowing the fireball's:
D_light = round(16.667 · 0.8·(Y/20)^0.44) + 40 ticks
One new constant, T_glow = 40 t = 2.4 s. It leaves the two largest weapons broadly as they are (+25% and +10%) and transforms the middle (×4.1 at 20 kt, ×2.5 at 100 kt). What NOT to change, and why.

  • Peak at tick 0 specifically. Moving the peak off tick 0 puts the ground up to TerrainRefreshInterval ticks behind the sprites at the brightest instant.

10 · Observation B — "new units come in and die instantly, long after the cloud is gone"

Reproduced on paper, and it is a real defect. The symptom I can prove: for every warhead of 100 kt and up, a lethal, actor-scanning effect is still running after the mushroom cloud sprite has finished playing — by 11 ticks at 100 kt, by 446 ticks (26.8 s) at 6 Mt and by 1483 ticks (89.0 s) at 50 Mt. During that window the effect damages any actor it finds inside its current radius, including actors that did not exist when the bomb went off. Nothing in either effect filters on "was this actor alive at detonation". The leading explanation, labelled as a hypothesis: the killer is the Warhead@BlastWave (ShockwaveDamage), not thermal radiation. The user's own hypothesis — lingering thermal — is also live in the same window and is also lethal to infantry, but it cannot one-shot a vehicle, and the report says units die instantly.

10.1 The mechanism, in the engine

Of the ~30 warheads on a nuclear weapon, exactly two spawn a world effect that keeps scanning for actors after impact. Everything else — every GrantExternalCondition fire band, every suppression band, the EMP, the smudges, the flash, the shakes — is a one-shot that fires at its own Delay and touches only what exists at that instant. Those are ruled out: a condition granted at tick 21 cannot reach a unit built at tick 400.

// engine/OpenRA.Mods.Common/Effects/ShockwaveEffect.cs - Tick() ticks++; radius += expansionPerTick * (1000 + excessPermille) / 1000; ... if (currentRadius > warhead.MaxRadius) { finished = true; ...remove...; return; } foreach (var victim in world.FindActorsOnCircle(center, currentRadius)) { if (hitActors.Contains(victim.ActorID)) continue; // ← the ONLY filter if (victim.IsDead || !victim.IsInWorld) continue; ... // Mark as hit (even if inside previous radius - they were missed, damage them now) hitActors.Add(victim.ActorID); warhead.ApplyBlastDamage(victim, firedBy, center, args); }

hitActors starts empty and is only ever added to. A unit created at tick 400 has an ActorID that has never been in it, so on the very next tick of the effect it is found by FindActorsOnCircle — which is a scan of the whole disc, not an annulus (WorldExtensions.cs:274) — and takes one full blast hit at the falloff for its distance from the epicentre. The comment on that line is explicit that hitting actors already inside the radius is intended; it was written for actors the wave overtook, and a newly-built unit is indistinguishable from one.

The thermal effect has the same shape and no filter at all:

// engine/OpenRA.Mods.Common/Effects/ThermalRadiationEffect.cs - Tick() ticksRemaining--; if (ticksRemaining <= 0) { ...remove...; return; } intervalCounter++; if (intervalCounter < warhead.DamageInterval) return; intervalCounter = 0; foreach (var victim in world.FindActorsOnCircle(center, warhead.MaxRange)) { if (victim.IsDead || !victim.IsInWorld) continue; warhead.ApplyThermalDamage(victim, firedBy, center, args); // ← no hit set at all: REPEATS }

10.3 Why the Supply Route is the worst case

SUPPLYROUTE carries ProductionFromMapEdge for Infantry, Soldier, Vehicle, Aircraft, Helicopter (structures.yaml:422–424): a bought unit is created at a map-edge cell near the closest SpawnArea and then walks to the rally point. Two things follow, and they compound:

  • The spawn cell is fixed and cannot dodge. It is one of SpawnCandidateCount: 5 cells on one map edge, chosen round-robin. If it is inside the wavefront's current radius, every unit bought lands in it.
  • The walk-in goes toward the epicentre, not away from it. The rally point defaults to the Supply Route itself, and the SR is at the player's base — which is where the bomb landed. A unit that survives its spawn cell walks inward along the falloff curve while the wave is still live.

10.4 Why it is invisible — the ring is drawn, but barely

The shockwave does render for its whole life. It is just very faint: ShockwaveOuterAlpha is 8 (percent) and the fade follows 1 − progress², so the ring is at 6% opacity halfway out and 1.5% at 90% out. Against a 12-second mushroom cloud that has just filled the screen, a 1.5%-alpha white band 5.5 cells thick is not something a player reads as "still lethal". Add that the cloud sprite finishes first and the flash is long gone, and the map looks finished while the most damaging warhead on the weapon is still expanding.

10.5 The number that controls it

MaxRadius on Warhead@BlastWave, divided by WaveSpeed. That quotient is the lifetime, because ShockwaveEffect ends on radius, not on a timer: expansionPerTick = 1024 / WaveSpeed and the effect is removed the tick radius > MaxRadius. At WaveSpeed: 7 that is 7 ticks per cell, 0.42 s per cell — so 102 cells costs 645 ticks and 246 cells costs 1682, and no other field shortens it. StartDelay adds to it; InitialSpeedPercent shaves a handful of ticks off the first cells and nothing after TransitionRadius.

Second number, for the thermal half: RadiationDuration on Warhead@ThermalRadiation.

The three shapes a fix could take, in increasing order of how much they change:

  • Engine, narrow: have ShockwaveEffect ignore actors whose Actor.ActorID is greater than the highest ID present when the effect was constructed — i.e. only damage actors that existed at detonation. One line in the constructor, one comparison in the loop. Same for ThermalRadiationEffect. This is the change that matches what a player expects a blast wave to be.
  • YAML, cheap: raise WaveSpeed on the big weapons so the wave finishes inside the cloud's 199 ticks. 102 cells in 199 ticks needs WaveSpeed: 1 — which is Mach 5 sustained and destroys the whole two-phase model. This does not actually work at 6 Mt or above, and it is worth saying so plainly.
  • YAML, blunt: cut MaxRadius. Remember it must be cut together with the Falloff table length × Spread, or the smaller of the two silently wins.

What would confirm the hypothesis in-game, if you want it confirmed before changing anything: fire a Strategic Nuclear Strike, then buy one unit and watch its health bar at a known tick. If the unit takes a single large hit the instant it appears, that is the shockwave. If it loses health in steps roughly every 8 ticks, that is the thermal effect. If it dies after tick 645 the explanation is neither and something else is running.

11 · Things that surprised me while reading

  • The mushroom cloud is the same length on every weapon. ScalePercent runs 56% to 1600% across the arsenal, but the sprite plays 107 frames on a fixed ChangeTick ladder and Animation.Tick() advances it 40 ms per game tick regardless of the 60 ms timestep. So a 0.3 kt cloud and a 50 Mt cloud both last 199 ticks. Everything else about a nuke scales with yield; this does not.
  • Infantry take 100% of blast damage and tanks take 40% — not because anyone wrote that, but because the Versus tables list Light/Medium/Heavy/Concrete and infantry armour is Kevlar or None, so no entry matches and DamageVersus falls through to a flat 100.
  • The two files derive RadiationDuration from two different laws. weapons-superweapons.yaml uses the thermal emission time (0.32·√Y s) — 24 ticks at 20 kt, 413 at 6 Mt. The arsenal file uses the fireball's incandescent life (the same D as the light) — so Tsar Bomba burns for 417 ticks where the emission law would say 1192. Both are documented and defended in their own file; they simply are not the same law. It is the 413 that puts thermal in the post-cloud window at 6 Mt.
  • The blast-wave lifetimes recorded in WORKSPACE/DISCOVERIES.md are stale. Its table says 89 / 218 / 427 / 594 / 1726 ticks for Atomic / W76 / Sarmat RV / AtomicHighYield / Tsar Bomba. Integrating the engine's own loop at 3d1251a4 gives 96 / 210 / 412 / 645 / 1682. The YAML comments agree with the integration, not with the bank (weapons-nuclear-arsenal.yaml says W76 "reaches its full 31.0c at tick 210"; the 50 kt entry says 166, which the integration also gives). The bank predates the 2026-09-06 two-phase shockwave rework.
  • Atomic's own header says its blast wave ends at tick 89. Its arrival list in the same comment says 15c at t96, and 15c0 is its MaxRadius. 89 is the 14-cell arrival — the SuppressionWind band's delay — not the end of the wave.
  • The 1.5 kt B61 dial is a fully-built weapon with no power wired to it. NukeB61Mod12Y015 exists in full, 298 lines of it, and nothing in nuclear-arsenal.yaml or player.yaml fires it.
