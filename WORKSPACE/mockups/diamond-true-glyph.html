The true shipped glyph, adapted to carry impediment and detection

Third attempt at this deliverable. The first varied where marks sit on the unit; the second varied the glyph's shape. Here the silhouette is fixed and the only variables are how it encodes two channels and how it gets its contrast.

Every mark on this page is the shipped font glyph, rasterised by FreeType out of the shipped FreeSansBold.ttf at the shipped 10 px, then masked and tinted. There is not one hand-drawn polygon in the output. U+2666 and U+25CA are loaded and filled; the engine's own contrast treatment is ported line for line from SpriteFont.CreateContrastGlyph. Built by WORKSPACE/mockups/diamond_true_glyph_assets.py; the glyph bitmaps and their coverage bytes are printed by glyph_probe.py. Judge at 1×.

The headline: the silhouette was never the problem

The previous pass hand-rasterised a diamond as an L1 ball and its own §4.4 flagged the result — "at 6×9 my diamond does not read as a diamond … this may be my rasteriser rather than the size." It was the rasteriser, but not in the way anyone expected.

Its 6×9 mask and the shipped U+2666 have the byte-identical row profile and the identical ink count. The footprint was already exactly right: 
previous hand-rasterised 6×9 : [2, 2, 4, 6, 6, 6, 4, 2, 2]  ink = 34 px
SHIPPED U+2666          6×9 : [2, 2, 4, 6, 6, 6, 4, 2, 2]  ink = 34 px
SHIPPED U+25CA          6×9 : [2, 3, 5, 4, 5, 4, 4, 3, 2]  ink = 32 px
What differs is the ink treatment, and it differs in two ways that together turn a diamond into a blob.
  1. Coverage. FT_LOAD_RENDER gives an 8-bit antialiased coverage bitmap (FreeTypeFont.cs:85). Of the shipped ♦'s 34 inked pixels only 16 are at ≥78% coverage; the tips run as low as 13/255. The previous pass drew every mask pixel at flat alpha 255. A solid 34 px lozenge at full opacity is a very different object from a soft one whose points fade out — the fade is what reads as a point.
  2. Contrast. The previous pass added a hard opaque 1 px keyline at rgba(0,0,0,200) on all eight neighbours. That is not what the engine does. UITextRenderable.Render (:57) calls DrawTextWithContrast(…, 1), which draws a greyscale dilation of the glyph's own alpha through CreateCircularWeightMap(1) — a soft halo with corner weight 0.60, derived from the antialiased coverage, one pixel up-left (SpriteFont.cs:302-417, :87). A hard ring of constant alpha thickens every edge equally; a dilation of a soft edge stays soft where the glyph is soft.
So: yes, the true glyph survives being filled at 6×9. It keeps its diamond identity from full down to roughly the halfway step. What does not survive is the empty end of the ladder — at fill step 0 a masked ♦ is a dark lump inside a black halo with no diamond identity at all (rows T1, T4, T6, T7, T8 below). Row T3 is the only one that solves it, and it solves it by doing what the game already does: using the real U+25CA lozenge for the bottom of the ladder.

The two glyphs are not one shape filled and unfilled

Nobody had checked this. They are different outlines. Both are 6×9, but 3 pixels of the lozenge lie outside the diamond suit altogether — U+25CA is a tall thin-stroked rotated square that bulges wider at the waist rows, U+2666 is a solid body with 2 px tips. They are characters from different Unicode blocks (Geometric Shapes vs Miscellaneous Symbols) and were chosen for availability, not for being a pair.

So "the same shape as the current one" is ambiguous, and this page resolves it explicitly: the silhouette everywhere below is U+2666. Three reasons, in order of weight: it is the only one of the two with an interior for a fill to march up; it is already the shipped silhouette for three of the five grades (SolidFromGrade: Moderate, defaults.yaml:958); and the lozenge's 1–2 px stroke is almost entirely partial coverage — only 4 of its 32 pixels reach ≥78% — so filling "inside" it means inventing interior pixels it does not have, which is drawing a new shape. The lozenge is not discarded: row T3 keeps it as the unfilled state, exactly as it ships.

A PITFALL re-confirmed rather than trusted. WithSpottedDecoration.cs:84-91 says U+25C6/U+25C7 — the obvious diamond pair — are absent from this font. They are. But the test has to be against .notdef, not against "did anything rasterise": FreeSansBold's .notdef is a 3×7 hollow rectangle carrying 16 px of ink, so an unmapped codepoint renders something. Both diamonds come out byte-identical to U+E000, U+0870 and U+4E00 — all .notdef. The comment is correct. A first pass of the probe asked the wrong question and briefly concluded it was wrong; that check is now in glyph_probe.py the right way round.

Proof strip — the shipped mark, then every variant, same ground

Detection 1–5 left to right, impediment held at 50. Row one is the shipped mark. This is what makes "the silhouette is preserved" checkable rather than asserted — if a row's outline departs from row one, it is visible here.

One channel is shipped code. The other is a document.

Detection is real and graded today. Detectable.CurrentVisibility is live, [Sync], recomputed every tick; Detectability.Grade inverts concealment into exposure and cuts the five bands Concealed / Low / Moderate / High / Spotted. Those five grades and their five colours are on screen in the game right now.
Impediment does not exist. WORKSPACE/impediment-spec.md §1.1 specifies I = clamp(0,100, S + D) and not one line of it is built — §1.4 and §7 list the engine work as unstarted. Every impediment value on this page is a number chosen to illustrate an encoding.

The standing rule (manager decision 08) is that you never draw a mark as a substitute for a mechanic that does not work. This page is exploration ahead of the mechanic, not instead of it — but the asymmetry is load-bearing: if any of these shipped before impediment did, the colour channel would be reporting a quantity the simulation cannot supply. Row T2 is the only variant fully drawable today, because it puts the channel that exists on the channel that carries five levels.

And on a vehicle the quantity underneath is degenerate. Concealment spans 3 of 9 levels on a vehicle and is permanently solid, so four of the five detection grades never fire there. Binary fill (row T7) is the only honest encoding on a vehicle today. That is a degenerate quantity under the mark, not a drawing problem — no variant can draw around it.

At rest, and with Shift held

User ruling, 2026-09-14: "Hide everything else for now unless shift is held — shift reveals all. We will redesign the stances, but for now keep them same and just hidden unless shift is held." Nothing is evicted; everything except the diamond is hidden at rest. The stance glyphs are drawn here exactly as they ship — same glyphs, same colours, same font, not restyled — because the ruling explicitly defers their redesign.

Cost is not scoped and is not quoted here. The layout plan priced "behind selection" as free YAML via RequiresSelection: true, but a held modifier key is a different mechanism and nobody has read the input path. Whether this is YAML, a small trait, or input-layer work is open.

Where this is still guessing

  1. Nothing has been seen in the running game. The brief withholds launching. Every level count is an eye on a browser composite and a PNG contact sheet.
  2. The ground is a real tile through a proxy palette. The tile is the genuine clear1.tem; temperat.pal is inside a Blowfish-encrypted local.mix, so it renders through plains.pal. True in-game ground is somewhat more olive. Since the question these marks must survive is contrast against ground, every contrast verdict here is provisional.
  3. PIL ships its own FreeType. Hinting matches in kind (FT_LOAD_DEFAULT both sides) and the ink box comes out 6×9 — the size the tree reports independently — but a hinted stem could in principle land one pixel differently from the game's freetype6.
  4. deviceScale is assumed 1.0. Every number in DecorationRowGeometry assumes it — GlyphFontSize is a bare 10 with no scale term. At a non-100% UI scale the glyph is re-rasterised larger and these bitmaps are not what is drawn.
  5. Nothing that moves can be settled in a still. A pulse on Spotted, a blink while suppression decays — plausible, none drawable here, so none proposed.
  6. The distribution is still unmeasured. impediment-spec.md §5.3 records that nobody knows how often a unit sits in each decile. Every count in the table is an upper bound on legibility, not a statement that the levels will be used.
