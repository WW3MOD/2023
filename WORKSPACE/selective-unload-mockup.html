Selective passenger unload — three interaction options

Scenario throughout: one APC holding 5 passengers — 2 Technicians and 3 riflemen. The player wants the technicians out and the riflemen kept aboard. Panels are drawn at 2× real size; every coordinate is doubled from the shipped geometry in mods/ww3mod/chrome/ingame-player.yaml (228×320 container, 12 px rows at Y = 18 + 14i, Tiny font).

Baseline — what ships today at main @ d1ad445d

Selective unload is not a missing feature. It is already built twice over: a per-row X that ejects one passenger, and a mark-then-deploy flow that sends chosen passengers to a clicked waypoint. Both already travel as proper orders. What is missing is any chance a stranger finds them.

Baseline. Four controls per row × 10 rows = 40 widgets. The two selective paths are X (eject this one) and [ ] → Deploy Marked. Nothing labels either.

Read this panel as a stranger. You have selected a transport. You see a checkbox with no caption, a letter R, a letter X, and three bottom buttons that sound similar — Mark All, Deploy Marked, Unload All Troops. Nothing tells you that X is how you get one man out, and nothing connects [ ] to Deploy Marked.

The mechanism underneath is already correct. X issues UnloadCargoPassenger carrying ExtraData = passenger.ActorID (CargoPanelLogic.cs:354), resolved in Cargo.ResolveOrder at Cargo.cs:350–357, which looks the actor up by ID and queues UnloadCargo(self, LoadRange, passenger). That is a fully replicated, per-passenger unload. It works today.

So the design question is not "how do we build this". It is "which affordance replaces [ ] R X", given the order underneath is already the right one.

The marks are client-local — and must stay that way. markedPassengerIds (CargoPanelLogic.cs:36) is mutated straight from click handlers with no order, and that is correct: it is never read by simulation (it reaches only CargoUnloadOrderGenerator, which issues real orders). It is intent, like unit selection. The eject-rally desync was a different animal — that dictionary lives in the Cargo trait and UnloadCargo reads it inside the simulation. Any design that "improves" marks by storing them on Cargo so they persist or replicate would create the very desync class we just fixed.

Option A — the passenger row is the button

Delete the checkbox and the X. Make each row one full-width button. Click a man, he gets out. One click, one complete action, no mode, no staging, nothing to remember.

A — after two clicks. Both Technicians are walking out; three riflemen still aboard, header already reads 3. Rows fade out as the unload activity runs rather than vanishing instantly, so a misclick is visible.

1 passenger: one row, one click — identical effort to Unload All, so the two never compete.

5 passengers: click the two Technicians. Two clicks, two orders, riflemen untouched. There is no counting, no staging step to forget, and no way to end up in a mode you did not know you entered.

Composes with unload-all: cleanly, because they are different verbs at different grain. Unload All Troops stays exactly as-is ("Unload", Cargo.cs:343). Row-click is the scalpel. Neither has state the other can corrupt.

Replicates as: the order that already exists, unchanged — new Order("UnloadCargoPassenger", transport, false) { ExtraData = passenger.ActorID }. No new order string, no wire-format change, no ProtocolVersion.Orders bump.

Cost: smallest of the three. In CargoPanelLogic.cs, move the existing EjectPassenger(slotIndex) handler off EJECT_CARGO_{i} onto a new full-width CARGO_ROW_{i} button; delete ToggleMark, IsPassengerMarked, CountValidMarks, markedPassengerIds and the MARK_ALL_CARGO handler. In YAML, each of the 10 rows loses two widgets and the label becomes a button. Net deletion.

Risk: a misclick ejects a man into a firefight, and given units are permanent budget loss that walked in from the map edge, that stings. Mitigated by the hover highlight, the explicit cursor text, and keeping the departing row visible while it leaves.

Option B — modifier + world radial (no panel involvement)

Attack the problem by removing the panel from the loop entirely. With the transport selected, hold Alt; passenger portraits fan out around the vehicle in the world. Click one, then click a cell — that man alone gets out and walks there.

B. Portraits fan around the selected APC while Alt is held. One is picked; the dashed line previews where he will be dropped.

1 passenger: heavy. Holding a modifier and aiming a radial to move one man is more ceremony than Unload All, which does the same thing with one right-click.

5 passengers: good — and it is the only option that puts the choice and the destination in one gesture, in the world, without ever looking at the sidebar.

Composes with unload-all: well. Plain deploy still unloads everyone; Alt is a strict refinement layered on the same actor.

Replicates as: two existing orders, in sequence on one transport — SetEjectRally (ExtraData + Target, Cargo.cs:358–365) to record the destination, then UnloadCargoPassenger to make him leave. Still no new order strings, but the sequencing is the exposure: an IOrderGenerator runs only on the machine holding the mouse, so every write between those two clicks must be an order and nothing may be stashed in the generator and applied later. This is precisely the shape that produced the live desync in EjectRallyOrderGenerator.

Cost: highest by a wide margin. A new IOrderGenerator with world-space portrait rendering, radial hit-testing, two-stage click state, cursor states, and correct cancel/deselect behaviour — plus the determinism review that shape now demands.

Fails the stated audience test outright. The brief asks for something a stranger finds by accident. A held modifier key is the canonical example of an interaction that cannot be discovered — it is invisible until taught. Listed because it was asked for and because it is genuinely the most powerful of the three, not because it fits the constraint.

Option C — keep marks, promote the waypoint, replace the ASCII

Commit to the two-step that already exists — mark, then deploy to a clicked waypoint — and fix its presentation: the mark becomes a lit row rather than [x], portraits replace the bare names, and Deploy Marked becomes the primary green action.

C. Two rows selected; the action button names the count and states what the next click does. The waypoint step is the point of the design, not an afterthought.

1 passenger: poor. Select a row, read a button, click the map — three deliberate acts to move one man, where Unload All does it in one.

5 passengers: the strongest of the three. Drop the two Technicians at the bridge while the transport keeps rolling with the riflemen aboard — a genuinely different tactical act that neither A nor a plain unload can express.

Composes with unload-all: worst of the three, and this is the existing complaint. The player now has three verbs — unload all, drop selected here, drop selected there — and the panel must make the difference legible without a manual.

Replicates as: unchanged and already correct — CargoUnloadOrderGenerator issues Move then one queued UnloadCargoPassenger per marked ID (CargoUnloadOrderGenerator.cs:103–110). Selection stays client-local in markedPassengerIds, which is the correct side of the line.

Cost: medium, and mostly art and YAML rather than C#. The logic already exists; what changes is row geometry, portrait assets, and the button copy. No new orders at all.

Recommendation: build A. Selective unload already works and already replicates correctly; the only thing actually broken is that nobody can find it. A is the only one of the three a stranger discovers by accident — a list of soldiers that lights up when you point at it — and it is the cheapest, because it reuses the existing order verbatim and deletes more code than it adds. Units in WW3MOD are permanent budget loss that walked in from the map edge, so the overwhelmingly common case is "get that one man out right now", not "orchestrate a two-waypoint drop"; A makes the common case one click and leaves the rare case to Unload All.

Keep C's waypoint flow as a second, clearly-labelled path if it is worth its complexity — but it should not be the thing a new player meets first. B should not be built: it is the most powerful and the most expensive, it is the one shape that just caused a live desync, and a held modifier key is disqualified by the audience constraint on its own.
