Release implementation plan

Drafted 2026-08-16 for review · main @ 81e5a440 · audit reports in WORKSPACE/audit/ · nothing implemented yet

The headline the plan is built around: a stranger cannot install or play WW3MOD today, and there is nothing for them to download. That is not a polish problem — it is the release itself. Everything else in this plan is ordered underneath it, except the things that are cheaper than the meeting it would take to discuss them.

Ranking rule used throughout (from your four answers): what a stranger hits, how early, how visibly — cost only as a tiebreak. Public-to-strangers audience; bot judged on “would a player screenshot this”; already-disabled content gets zero effort.

Phase 2 — Make it installable

This is the actual release gate. Until it is done, every other improvement is invisible to the audience you chose. It is also the phase most likely to surprise us, so it goes early rather than last.

  • BLOCKER The packaged game would be missing the ra mod it hard-depends on. mod.yaml:20 mounts $ra: ra non-optionally plus eight further paths through it; the packaging scripts never copy mods/ra because PACKAGING_COPY_ENGINE_FILES is empty at mod.config:104. Result: Could not load mod 'ra' before the menu ever appears.
  • BLOCKER The Red Alert content installer is dead configuration. mod.yaml:13 declares DefaultFileSystem, which does not implement the interface the installer gates on — so the hand-written install prompt can never display, while every .mix mount fails silently.
  • BLOCKER No artifact has ever been built. CI targets macos-11 and windows-2019, both retired runner images.
  • SHOULD-FIX Identity through the install chain: install dir, registry key, Start Menu folder, the crash dialog pointing at wiki.openra.net, and the mod hotlinking OpenRA’s stock Red Alert logo as its web icon.

Gate out of Phase 2 — the one run that matters: launch once with %APPDATA%/OpenRA/Content renamed aside. Nothing here is proven until that happens, because this machine has always had the prerequisite already satisfied. That is why the bug survived.

Phase 3 — Unblock your parallel work

Deliberately early, and this is the one ordering choice I would most like you to sanity-check. Art and audio are yours, they are the longest-lead items in the whole release, and you cannot start until you know what is missing. Handing you the lists early buys weeks of parallelism; handing them over at the end serialises the release behind them.

  • U9 — art + audio TODO lists. The content-completeness audit is producing the per-actor matrix now: cameos, descriptions, voice sets, firing sounds, death FX, with counts and a “wire an existing asset” vs “needs new art” split. It arrives as a work list, not a report.
  • U4 — command bar icons. Already costed: 19 of 25 buttons share art; 11 distinct sprites; you need 14 new icons. Worst offender is stance-icons/defend at five uses, and resupply is a literal alias of deploy with a TODO already attached. Format is friendly — glyphs.png is 256×256 RGBA, no palette, no SHP step.
Your call, and I have a recommendation. On placeholder icons: I can generate 24×24 distinct geometric glyphs plus disabled variants and wire them. I would rather not. They will sit next to hand-drawn glyphs and look generated — and on a first-impression surface, duplicate icons read as a design choice while mismatched placeholders read as unfinished. My pick: ship duplicates, use the table as your work list. Middle path available: placeholders for only the five worst offenders.

Phase 5 — Design work you asked to be involved in

These are the two you explicitly said you might want hands on. Deliverable is a proposal plus mockups you sign off, not code landed behind your back.

  • U6 — selective passenger unload. Design + mockups. Wants designing together with R10, since both touch how cargo unload orders are issued, and R10 is already changing that code.
  • U5 — garrison usability. Research + ideas. One concrete defect already found: the graduated suppression “duck” is computed every tick at GarrisonManager.cs:641 and read by nothing — repo-wide grep finds only writes. So suppression currently reads as binary, which may be a large part of why garrison feels unintuitive.
  • Lexicon / guide tab (your idea from the U10 message) — folded in here as a design item rather than a quick fix.

Phase 6 — Bot: stop the screenshot-worthy stupidity

Scoped by your “somewhere in between” answer. These are the visible symptoms you reported from live play. The deep architecture — danger-scale rework, benchmark re-baseline — is explicitly not in the release path.

  • 66 — soldiers sit dry while almost no supply trucks are bought. Your ruling stands: resupply is the top procurement precedence at all times.
  • 63 — the opening builds two medics before any line infantry.
  • 64 — the first tank pushes out alone.

These three are one disease: the system has a notion of how many and no notion of when. Fixing quantities buys a third report with a different unit, so the work is the missing ordering axis — which also makes it a single change rather than three.

Phase 7 — Launch

  • U8 — ModDB page and copy, GitHub landing page, screenshots, trailer/gif shortlist, release notes. Sequenced last on purpose: announcement copy is worthless until the download works, and screenshots are worthless until Phases 3–4 land.

What I am NOT proposing to do

  • Danger-scale rework (item 40) and the @stable benchmark re-baseline (item 43). Long-standing top-of-queue items, dropped out of the release path by your bot answer. They stay in the pipeline as the principled versions of real problems.
  • Anything already disabled — hidden units, shelved airstrike powers, the two dead build queues’ actors. Zero effort, per your instruction.
  • Stances, Supply Route contestation, three-mode move. The tracker lists them open; the audit found all three complete. No work, just a tracker correction.
  • The 49 backlog entries that were fixed and never struck off, and the 9 that were never bugs. Reconciliation only.

How I would run it

  • Batching: one worker per subsystem, not per item — Phase 4’s chrome items are one branch, not eight. Isolated worktrees under C:\Users\fredr\worktrees\ww3mod\, adversarial reviewer for behavioural changes, manager merges on green and pushes main.
  • Simulation: centralised with me, per your instruction. The seven-day budget resets shortly; measurement-heavy work is scheduled after that reset, and no worker starts a run on its own.
  • Verification: Phase 2’s gate (launch with the content dir renamed aside) is the single highest-value run in this plan and I would spend the first launch on it.
  • Tracks: one per item, as you asked — U1–U10 plus the audit slices are already live in Maestro.
The three things I would most like your view on:
  1. Is Phase 3 in the right place? Giving you the art/audio lists before fixing the first-ten-minutes bugs is a deliberate bet on parallelism. If you would rather see the game clean up first, it moves down and nothing else changes.
  2. Placeholder icons — generate or not? My recommendation is not to, and to hand you the duplicate table instead.
  3. Phase 6 sizing. The three bot items are one root cause; fixing it properly is a real change, not a tweak. If that feels too big for the release, the alternative is three narrow symptom fixes that I expect will regress.
