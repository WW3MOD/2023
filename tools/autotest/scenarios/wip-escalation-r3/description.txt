WIP: DEFCON 3 staging captures -- what the bots do with the no-rush period.

R3 of the 2026-09-19 Escalation review (WORKSPACE/audit/
escalation-gameplay-review-260919.md, "Runs requested"). A SCRATCH CAPTURE RIG,
not a gate scenario: it measures nothing and asserts nothing about the mode.
Delete it once the frames have been read.

WHY IT EXISTS. The review's §2.7 ends with three questions about the bots under
Escalation that could be READ out of the configuration but not SEEN:

  1. does the border staging produce a LINE or a CLUMP?
  2. is artillery held BEHIND the staged line at standoff, or parked in it?
  3. do the slack minutes of the clock go on DEFENSES, or on more armour at the
     border?

All three are visual. The manager's screenshot channel drives
TestModeScreenshots through a cmd file whose vocabulary is `screenshot`,
`click` and `hover` -- it cannot move the camera -- so a scenario that frames
itself is the only way to answer them.

IT IS test-escalation-full-match, UNCHANGED BUT FOR THE SCRIPT. map.bin is the
same file (md5 90824f8f1840b1c7ce4fc628da067db5, identical to
mods/ww3mod/maps/polar-disorder-ww3/map.bin), map.yaml differs only in its
Title, and rules.yaml differs only in the LuaScript line -- including the
TimeLimitTicks and RunInTestMode departures this rig never reaches, since it
writes its verdict at tick 5200. That is deliberate rather than lazy: GIVEN
THE SAME --seed, R1 AND R3 RUN THE SAME MATCH, so these frames are pictures of
the match R1 puts tick stamps on. Both Lua scripts are read-only pollers that
issue no orders, create no actors and draw no shared random; Camera.Position and
Camera.Zoom are client-local view state that no ISync type reads
(ViewportIsNotSimulationStateTest asserts that by IL scan).

THE GEOMETRY IS DERIVED, NOT EYEBALLED. Decoded offline from this scenario's own
map.bin via tools/nav-guard/modload.py. The homes are 93,16 and 4,81, 110.2
cells apart, so the derived bisector runs north-west to south-east through
48.5,48.5. Walking it cell by cell shows it is NOT one open line: the midpoint
is a four-cell CLIFF plug, and there are Rock+Water belts further out. The two
wide crossings near the direct axis are cells (35,30)..(47,46) and
(50,50)..(62,67), 21 cells each, both carrying a Road. Their centres 41,38 and
56,59 are where the close frames aim. The full belt table is in the Lua header.

**A CLUMP AT A GAP IS THEREFORE CORRECT PLAY, NOT A DEFECT**, and that caveat is
the single most important thing to carry into reading the images. A force
massed on a road crossing is funnelling through the only ground the terrain
offers. What would be wrong is a clump on open frontage with twenty empty cells
beside it, or a force strung along a belt it can never cross.

TWELVE FRAMES: THREE MOMENTS x FOUR SCALES/VIEWPOINTS, so any two frames sharing
a letter are directly comparable.

  01-t2500-*     half way through Positioning (tick 2500 of 5000)
  02-t4800-*     twelve seconds before the border lifts (tick 4800)
  03/04-t50xx-*  10 to 90 ticks after it lifts (5010, 5040, 5070, 5100)

  -a-wide-whole-border  fully out. Camera.MinZoom is 0.25 here -- WW3MOD leaves
                        Viewport.unlockMinZoom on and unlockedMinZoomScale is
                        0.25 -- so this spans four times the linear extent of
                        zoom 1 and shows the whole map. WHERE each side massed,
                        and the insurance frame if they massed nowhere near the
                        direct axis.
  -b-mid-both-gaps      zoom 1, ordinary playing magnification, on 48,48. Both
                        open crossings and the cliff plug in one frame. THE
                        PRIMARY FRAME for line-vs-clump, and the fallback if the
                        fully-out frame is too small to count and the close pair
                        too tight to show frontage.
  -c-nw-gap / -d-se-gap zoom 2, centred ON the border at 41,38 and 56,59, so
                        both armies' near-border ground is in one frame. The
                        artillery-standoff frames: a held battery reads as a
                        second rank with a gap in front of it, and that gap is
                        only measurable against the line it sits behind.

THREE SCALES RATHER THAN ONE BECAUSE THE MANAGER RUNS THIS ONCE. Picking a single
magnification would be a gamble on a viewport whose cell coverage depends on the
window resolution; three means at least one frame per moment is legible.

THE VERDICT ONLY GUARDS INTERPRETABILITY. At each Positioning capture tick it
requires Test.DefconLevel() == 3 and Test.DefconWallActive() == true. If
Escalation failed to apply, the level reads 0 and twelve photographs of an
ordinary Skirmish would be indistinguishable from twelve photographs of the thing
we wanted. There is no assertion at tick 5010 on purpose: the review predicts
DEFCON 2 may already be over by then, and that is the finding rather than a
fault. Calling Test.Pass is also what FLUSHES the PNGs --
TestGlobal.ExitWhenCapturesFlushed polls AllCapturesFlushed() before exiting,
and a run killed by --timeout can lose captures still mid-encode on the
ThreadPool.

EACH CENSUS LINE IN THE VERDICT CARRIES NUMBERS BESIDE THE SHAPES: ground
attackers within 12 cells of each gap, per side, plus each side's total. "Line
or clump" is a question about how N units are arranged, and a reviewer should
not have to count N off a photograph.

RUN LINE. --background is required (a --hidden run never maps the window and
writes no PNGs), and --seed MUST be R1's OWN RECORDED SEED or the
frames are of a different match. R1 ran with the harness's default
DateTime.Now seed and result.json recorded it as -1662796604 (run
260920_010605_p1901); run-test.sh accepts a negative integer:

    ./tools/autotest/run-test.sh --background --speed 8 --seed -1662796604 \
        --timeout 240 wip-escalation-r3

The verdict lands at tick 5200, which at --speed 8 is about 40 s of wall clock
plus startup -- so 240 is generous rather than tight, and a run that reaches the
timeout instead of writing a verdict failed to reach tick 5200 for a reason worth
reading.

If the wide frames come back too small to pick out individual vehicles, re-run
with --size 1920x1200; MinZoom is derived from the window resolution, so a
bigger window fits the same map into more pixels rather than showing more map.
