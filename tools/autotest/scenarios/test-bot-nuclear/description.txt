TEST: the bot fires only when it is losing, and takes the biggest band it may.

Two players on a 66x34 map, DEFCON Escalation, starting at DEFCON 1 with the
first-warheads clock compressed to ten ticks and all four side cooldowns
compressed to 300 ticks (shipped: 5000/7000/9000/12000). USA is scripted; Russia
is a `Bot: experimental` and is the thing under test. Nobody has any money, so no
reinforcement can be called in and army value moves only when the script moves
it.

Eight identical humvees, four each -- the cheapest actor in the mod that counts
toward ArmyValue, at 500 credits -- so both sides start at exactly 2000 and the
army ratio is exactly 100 %. Killing three of Russia's makes it exactly 25 %,
against the module's LosingArmyRatioPercent of 60.

  PHASE A (t90)    Release has opened and Russia's 1 kt power reads READY. The
                   bot must NOT have fired. This is the user's whole rule for
                   bots -- "It only fires if it is losing, so it never escalates
                   unnecessarily" -- and the bin reading is what makes the zero
                   mean something: a bot with an empty bin cannot demonstrate
                   restraint.

  PHASE A2 (t280)  Still zero, four evaluations later. One quiet reading could
                   be a module that had not started ticking.

  PHASE B (t300)   USA fires its 0.3 kt B61 at empty ground. Nothing is aimed at
                   anything: the exchange raises the other side's level wherever
                   the warhead lands, with no damage attribution at all.

  THE FLIGHT       ~305 ticks, and the run WATCHES it rather than waiting out a
  (t300..~t605)    constant. Since the 2026-09-16 ruling the victim's level does
                   not rise on the click: it rises at the first warhead's IMPACT
                   plus EscalationDelayTicks (50). Two assertions live in that
                   window, and they are the coverage this scenario adds --
                   nothing else in the tree ran the deferral end to end.

                     (a) Russia's 20 kt stays HIDDEN on every tick from the
                         launch until Test.GetImpactEffectCount moves. RED the
                         moment an escalation is applied at the click, which is
                         exactly what this scenario's deleted
                         `EscalationDelayTicks: -1` override restored.

                     (b) and is drawn within 50 + 40 ticks of that explosion.
                         RED if the deferral never fires at all -- a dropped
                         pending record, an impact never reported. (a) cannot
                         see that: a level that never rises is also a level
                         that never rises early.

  PHASE B2         THE SHARPEST ASSERTION IN THE RUN. Russia's 20 kt power now
  (rise + 12)      reads READY -- its level rose to 2 -- and the bot must STILL
                   not have fired. A level is PERMISSION, not a REASON. A bot
                   that fires here has the engine's generic SupportPowerBotModule
                   policy ("ready and a target scores well enough"), which is
                   precisely the shape the user's rule rejects.

                   THIS GOT STRONGER AT v2. A retaliation window expired, so a
                   bot that held through one might merely not have got round to
                   firing. A level does not expire: this bot can take the 20 kt
                   at any point for the rest of the match, and declines every
                   time it is asked.

                   AND STRONGER AGAIN WITH THE DEFERRAL: the bot now spends the
                   whole 305-tick flight -- six evaluations -- holding a loaded
                   1 kt with a warhead inbound, and then declines the 20 kt too.

                   READ 12 TICKS AFTER THE BAND APPEARS, not 80 after the rise.
                   The anchor is the tick the condition arrived, so only one
                   ServicePendingReady pass is owed; and 12 is far inside the
                   300-tick interval an UNGRANTED timer would be running, which
                   is what keeps READY non-vacuous.

  PHASE C          Three of Russia's four humvees are killed. 500 against 2000
  (rise + 32)      is 25 %. Three and not four: a losing player and an
                   annihilated one are different scenarios.

  PHASE D (C+300)  Exactly ONE launch, at band 2 (TwentyKiloton -- the highest
                   band its LEVEL allows, not the 1 kt underneath) with reason
                   "HighestAllowed". The earliest legal launch is three
                   evaluations after the kills (LosingStreakRequired 3 x
                   EvaluationInterval 50 = 150 ticks); this reading sits three
                   further intervals past that.

  PHASE E (C+330)  And the bot's WHOLE arsenal went down with the shot -- its
                   1 kt as well as the 20 kt it fired. A cooldown is side-wide.
                   Under v1 this read READY and a losing bot worked down its
                   ladder firing one warhead per band.

  PHASE F (C+660)  And it comes back. Asserted so phase G means something: a bot
                   that never recovered would also never fire again.

  PHASE G (C+760)  Still exactly one launch -- now on MinTicksBetweenLaunches
                   ALONE, because phase F just proved the arsenal is loaded. That
                   separation is why the cooldown override is 300 rather than
                   something still running at this tick.

Every offset from PHASE C on is the one this scenario used when the army cut was
a fixed t400; only the ANCHOR moved, from a constant to the observed level rise.
C above is the army-cut tick.

WHY THE LAUNCH COUNT IS A BINDING AND NOT A BIN READ. A launch shows up in the
support power bin only as a band going READY -> CHARGING, which a side cooldown
and a launch both produce. "Exactly once" is not a question the bin can answer. Test.GetBotNuclearState reads
NuclearBotModule.LaunchCount, which is the count of orders the module actually
queued. The bin is still read in phases A and B2, for the one thing it is the
right instrument for.

WHAT THIS DELIBERATELY DOES NOT MEASURE:
  * The fog discipline. The module takes its targets from BeliefStore, which is
    fog-legal by construction, but this run has fog OFF and the map pre-explored
    so that the bot's candidate set is knowable. The fog path is exercised and
    never stressed; a scenario that measured it would be a different scenario.
  * The aim point. The bot's 20 kt lands on USA's humvee cluster because that is
    where the value is, but nothing here asserts the cell. PickAimIndex is pinned
    without a world by NuclearPolicyMathTest.
  * The game-ender path and the final exchange. Reaching either needs a chain of
    replies at 50 kt and 100 kt, which is a much longer run; both branches are
    pinned by NuclearPolicyMathTest instead.
  * The losing predicate's contestation half. Nothing contests Russia's Supply
    Route here, so the army ratio is the only live term. Deliberate: two live
    terms in one OR would make a failure ambiguous.

THE SOFTEST ASSUMPTION IN THE RUN is that the two armies do not shoot each
other. They sit fifty cells apart with HoldFire / HoldPosition on ^Combatant,
and the whole run is ~1450 ticks -- but Russia IS a bot, and its ordinary ground
modules are free to order its humvees anywhere. If phase C ever reports fewer
than three kills, that is what happened, and the fault text says so rather than
letting the run fail somewhere downstream as a mystery.
