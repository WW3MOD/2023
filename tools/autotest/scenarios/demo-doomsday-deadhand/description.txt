DEMO: the FINAL EXCHANGE -- the clock runs out, both sides get fifteen seconds to place their own
game-enders, an unplaced package fires at the enemy anyway, and the map is annihilated.

RETIMED 2026-09-20 WITH THE REDESIGN. The map-wide "Dead Hand" salvo this demo used to show is
gone: it was side-blind and it always landed before the player's own warhead. What is shown now is
TWO PACKAGES ON ONE CASCADE -- USA's, placed, and Russia's, fired for it at USA's half of the map
-- with nothing landing until the window has shut. The directory keeps its name because a scenario
directory name is its run identifier.

IT CAPTURES FIVE FRAMES AND ENDS ITSELF on Test.Skip once they have flushed, so run-test.sh returns
a verdict (SKIP, exit 2) rather than killing the game at its watchdog. It is still a DEMO: nothing
is asserted, and `skip` is the run saying "I finished, the frames are on disk, a person decides" --
not a claim that anything passed. The two runs before 2026-09-13 took no screenshots and never
ended, which is why they came back TIMEOUT-FAIL with an empty lua.log and no PNGs.

Watch for, in order: the countdown band ("FINAL EXCHANGE -- PLACE YOUR STRIKE PACKAGE -- UNPLACED
FIRES AT THE ENEMY -- 0:15") and a B83 cameo appearing in a bin that was empty; USA's own two 1.2 Mt
bombs going up inside the window with beacons around the eastern city; the band clearing at zero and
"Fired automatically for: Russia."; then a long quiet while everything flies; then FOUR impacts
fifteen ticks apart -- USA's two first, Russia's two after -- and then everything still standing is
destroyed.

THE PACKAGE HERE IS TWO PER SIDE, and that is the MinPackage floor doing its work: 64x32 is 2048
playable cells, which rounds to 1 at the shipped CellsPerImpact 2400 and is lifted to 2. A bigger
map gets a bigger package from the same arithmetic; see FinalExchangePackageTest for the table.

RUN LENGTH IS ~700 TICKS, up from ~545 (2026-09-13) and still well down on the ~780 before that.
The extra 155 is the cascade's anchor: nothing of the exchange may land until the window has shut
AND a full flight has been flown, which is the property the redesign exists to guarantee, so it
cannot be compressed away -- only the flight it waits for can be, and it has been (MissileDelay 500
-> 120 on both game-enders, FinalExchangeFlightTicks 800 -> 260). Two runs were once CPU-starved by
sibling builds and reached under 100 ticks in 300 s, which nothing in this scenario can fix. THE
WINDOW ITSELF WAS NOT CUT and must not be: 250 ticks is the shipped fifteen seconds and is the
thing being demonstrated. On a loaded machine, prefer
`--speed 4` (run-test.sh divides the timestep, so the band still honestly reads 0:15 while the
wall clock shrinks -- it converts with GameSpeed.Timestep, which --speed does not touch) and
`--timeout 600`.

WHAT WAS GIVEN UP WITH THE SHORTER RUN-UP, stated rather than quietly dropped: at 250 ticks the
three USA units reliably killed the lone Russian BMP before the freeze, so USA led on SCORE while
Russia had more units ALIVE -- score and survival deliberately disagreeing, which is the
discrimination the mode exists to make. At 80 ticks that kill may not land. Nothing is lost from
what this demo can SHOW, because the frozen-score verdict is unshowable under TestMode either way
(see the caveat); it is lost from what a reader could infer about why the mode freezes at all.

WHAT TO DO IF USA'S WARHEAD NEVER FLIES. The on-screen line at tick 95 says why, and the same
string is carried into the Test.Skip note and into frames 02 and 05: "not-ready:0" means the power
was never armed (the window did not grant it), "hidden" means the bin never drew it,
"unknown-power:B83Strike" means the arsenal is not loaded. Nothing is asserted here, so that
printout is the only thing standing between a silent miss and a wasted launch slot.

NO WAVES AT ALL ANY MORE. This paragraph used to explain the missing third wave (the fill pass,
removed in the 2026-09-07 retune). The other two went on 2026-09-20 with the salvo itself: there is
no outlier wave, no pause and no city wave, because there is no map-wide salvo to structure. What
replaced them is ONE cascade of evenly-spaced slots, and what decides its length is how many
warheads the two packages carry. Ground between the aim points is uncovered on purpose, and the
sweep at the end is what makes "nothing survives" true.

CAVEAT -- THE VERDICT IS NOT SHOWN HERE. A demo runs under TestMode, where ConquestVictoryConditions
deliberately never assigns an objective (ConquestVictoryConditions.cs, the TestMode guard in Tick),
so the win/loss resolution is a no-op and no "USA is victorious" line appears. The window, the
player-placed warhead, the freeze, the split, the ordering and the annihilation are all exercised;
the frozen-score verdict is the one stage this demo cannot show, and it needs a normal skirmish
launch to see.
