Demo: the high-yield strategic nuke detonates at cell 64,80 on a fully-explored map with fog ON and NO camera actor, so the whole top map edge sits at uniform visibility 1 -- maximum fog, which is where the fog-piercing restoration does the most work (a fogged cell transmits 0.1378 at the shipped FogDarkness 1.4, so 86% of the light there is restored rather than drawn directly). Ground zero is eighty cells from the edge ON PURPOSE: the light is saturated white for roughly its first forty-five cells, and two saturated rows either side of a boundary look identical whatever the renderer is doing, so the edge has to sit in the falloff. The camera is parked on the EDGE, not on ground zero, and zoomed to 3x the fully-out level so one cell row is tens of pixels tall. WHAT TO LOOK FOR: the last row of ground before the black -- the one-cell unplayable ring that every shipped map carries between Bounds and MapSize -- must be continuous with the row below it, dimmer only by the light's own falloff over one cell, which is the same small step every neighbouring pair of rows shows. A single row plainly darker than BOTH its neighbours is the bug this demo exists to show; before the fix it was several times darker, because the restoration was clipped to the playable Bounds while the fog it compensates for is drawn a cell wider. WHAT IS NOT THE BUG, and it will mislead a viewer who does not read this: the black BEYOND that ring must NOT light up. WorldRenderer.DrawBeyondMapFog paints that region opaque black by design, before actors are drawn, and the faint halo visible over it is the mushroom cloud SPRITE rather than lit ground. If terrain ever appears out there, that is a real bug in the other direction. The no-leak guarantee is unchanged and is the thing to check second: a ring cell borrows its fog mask from the one playable cell it abuts, so on a map where that cell is unexplored the ring must still be black.
