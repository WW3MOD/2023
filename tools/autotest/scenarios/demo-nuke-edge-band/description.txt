Demo: the high-yield strategic nuke detonates at cell 64,80 on a fully-explored map with fog ON and NO camera actor, so the whole top map edge sits at uniform visibility 1 -- maximum fog, which is where the fog-piercing restoration does the most work (a fogged cell transmits 0.1378 at the shipped FogDarkness 1.4, so 86% of the light there is restored rather than drawn directly). Ground zero is eighty cells from the edge ON PURPOSE: the light is saturated white for roughly its first forty-five cells, and two saturated rows either side of a boundary look identical whatever the renderer is doing, so the edge has to sit in the falloff. The camera is parked on the EDGE, not on ground zero, and zoomed to 3x the fully-out level so one cell row is tens of pixels tall. WHAT TO LOOK FOR: the last row of ground before the black -- the one-cell unplayable ring that every shipped map carries between Bounds and MapSize -- must be continuous with the row below it, dimmer only by the light's own falloff over one cell, which is the same small step every neighbouring pair of rows shows. A single row plainly darker than BOTH its neighbours is the bug this demo exists to show; before the fix it was several times darker, because the restoration was clipped to the playable Bounds while the fog it compensates for is drawn a cell wider. AMENDED 2026-09-09 (wt/beyond-bounds-light): the black BEYOND that ring IS now expected to light up, and the sentence that used to call that a bug is gone. "Nothing may put light on it" was a real design rule -- WorldRenderer.DrawBeyondMapFog fills that region opaque black before actors are drawn -- and the user has overridden it, because a nuke lighting the ground up to a hard rectangle while its own fire went on burning outside it is what they reported. BeyondMapLightRenderable now continues the same light on the same cell lattice past the grid, from the above-shroud slot so the beyond-map fog strips cannot dim it below the on-grid value, so the grid boundary should show the light's own falloff over one cell and nothing else -- the same small step every neighbouring row shows. WHAT MUST NOT APPEAR OUT THERE IS TERRAIN: the region has no cells and no tiles, so a smooth coloured glow on black is right, and any texture, tile edge or actor becoming visible is a real bug in the other direction. The no-leak boundary is unchanged but is NOT exercised by this demo, and a viewer should not read a pass here as covering it: this map is pre-explored, so every border cell is explored and the glow is expected all along the edge. Where a border cell has never been scouted the glow beyond it must be absent -- the mask is read through the same ShroudRenderer.ClampToPlayable the shroud itself uses -- and that case is pinned in BeyondMapLightTest rather than in a frame. The no-leak guarantee is unchanged and is the thing to check second: a ring cell borrows its fog mask from the one playable cell it abuts, so on a map where that cell is unexplored the ring must still be black.
