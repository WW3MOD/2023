Demo: the six screen-shake profiles fired in sequence at a fixed camera (cell 64,100), each announced on screen before it lands. Shell (t=50), building collapse (t=150), heavy impact (t=270), tactical nuke at the camera (t=420), the SAME tactical nuke 40 cells away (t=760), high-yield nuke (t=1450); the run ends around t=2200 (132 s). Watch the camera, not the map - the probes are infantry whose death explosion does no damage and has no visual, so the only thing that happens is the viewport moving. Stages 4 and 5 are the comparison the demo exists for: at the camera the nuke is an immediate 12 px hit; 40 cells out its ground wave takes 2.2 s to arrive at 1.9 px and its air-blast stage turns up 18.7 s after detonation. Stage 5 is faint on purpose - it is about the two arrival times and the size of the drop, not the size of the shake. Screen shake cannot be judged from a screenshot; for the numeric shape of these same profiles run the NUnit fixture ScreenShakeModelTest.TabulateShippedProfiles with console verbosity detailed.
